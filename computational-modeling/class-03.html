Step 3 of 7 · A reminder from calculus.

A drawn pulse, not measured. Area below the axis counts as negative.

from calculus$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$ The signed area between $f$ and the axis, where $F' = f$ (the fundamental theorem).
in the crash test$\int_0^{t_1} a(t)\,dt = v(t_1) - v(0)$ Since $a = dv/dt$, the velocity is an antiderivative of the acceleration. The area under the acceleration record is the change in velocity.
unitsm/s² × s = m/s Each strip of area is a height (m/s²) times a width (s), so the area is a velocity. Since $v = dx/dt$, the area under a velocity is a distance.

An integral of sampled data adds up the area of a shape fitted between each pair of samples.

Step 3 of 7 · A reminder from the notes.

A drawn curve, not measured. Area below the axis counts as negative. The full page is Area under a curve.

T − R = ?(h/2)[f(xn) − f(x0)]
T = h[ ½f0 + f1 + … + fn−1 + ½fn ]
R = h[ f0 + f1 + … + fn−1 ] ← same middle terms
T − R = h[ ½fn − ½f0 ] = (h/2)[f(xn) − f(x0)]
On the Civic: h = 0.0001 s
f(x0) = 1.14, f(xn) = 13.81 m/s²
T − R = (0.0001/2)(13.81 − 1.14) = 0.00063 m/s
rectangles$R = h \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} f(x_i)$ Each panel's area is its width h times its left-hand sample. The error falls in proportion to h.
trapezoids$T = h\left[\frac{f(x_0)}{2} + f(x_1) + \dots + f(x_{n-1}) + \frac{f(x_n)}{2}\right]$ Each panel's area is h times the average of its two samples, so the end samples count half. The error falls with h², so halving h quarters it.
on a measured recordh is the sample interval The header sets it (0.1 ms on the Civic), so it cannot be made smaller. The trapezoid rule costs one extra add and multiply, so it is the usual choice.

Step 5 of 7 · A reminder from the notes.

Drawn, not measured, with the offset made large enough to see. The full page is Bias and noise.

the recordai = si + b + εi The signal s is the motion we want. The offset b is the same in every sample, and the noise ε differs from sample to sample. Only the sum is recorded.
what can be doneOne is a number, one is a spread Integrated, the offset gives $\int_0^T b\,dt = bT$, one number. The noise partly cancels as it is summed.
nextWhere s is zero Before contact the car is towed at a steady speed, so the record there holds only b + ε.

Before contact the record should read zero, and its average is −0.043 m/s².

Step 5 of 7 · Could our methods be responsible for the gap?

What we found: the mean is the offset, and the scatter about it is the noise, with a standard deviation of 1.75 m/s².

b = −0.043 m/s², T = 0.30 s
bT = −0.043 × 0.30 = −0.013 m/s
σ = 1.75 m/s², Δt = 0.0001 s
σ√(Δt T) = 1.75 × √(0.0001 × 0.30)
≈ 0.010 m/s
what we learnThe three sums, from contact to the end $\Delta t\sum a_i = \Delta t\sum s_i + \Delta t\sum b + \Delta t\sum \varepsilon_i$
  • Signal. $\Delta t\sum s_i = \Delta v$, the change we want.
  • Offset. $n b\,\Delta t = bT$, growing with the record's length.
  • Noise. Partly cancels, typically $\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t\,T}$.
On the Civic each is about 1% of the 1.13 m/s gap.
nextCan we take them out? The offset and the noise behave differently when they are summed.

The offset can be subtracted, because it is the same in every sample.
