A Memo

Example 1 · Memo

To: Bob

Re: Which flight model to build the season on

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Build on the model that holds gravity at 9.81 m/s². I fitted both models to the first 10 frames of free flight (0.4 s, at 25 frames a second) on 23 of the 24 training arcs, and asked each one how high the ball would be when it reached the hoop. Holding gravity, the prediction was off by 4.3 cm on the median arc and by 18.1 cm at worst. Fitting gravity, it was off by 16.3 cm on the median arc and by 180.8 cm at worst. The fixed model was closer on 19 of the 23 arcs.

The model that fits gravity is better at one thing, which is passing close to the 10 frames it was given. Its residual (the rms gap between the curve and those frames) was smaller on all 23 arcs, but only just, at 1.74 cm against 1.79 cm on the median arc. Both are under the 3 cm of noise the tracker is said to have. The free model probably gets its small advantage by bending its curve to that noise, and the gravity it reports ranges from 5.33 to 11.86 m/s² across the arcs. That bend is likely why its prediction drifts away from the ball once the frames run out. Figure 1 shows a typical arc.

How I decided. The file does not say when the ball leaves the hand, so I estimated the vertical acceleration at each frame from a parabola through the 9 frames around it. While the hand is pushing, the acceleration is upward. I started each flight 5 frames after the last upward reading, so that no estimate there reaches back into the throw. I ended each flight at the last frame where the ball was still 1 m or more from the hoop, measured across the floor, before it can touch the rim. The error at the hoop is the gap between the predicted and the measured height at that last frame.

Three things need your attention.

  • Arc 325 has a filled-in gap. From near the top of the flight to the hoop its height falls in straight lines, by the same step every frame. Real tracking always carries some noise, so the tracker probably lost the ball and the gap was filled in. I left this arc out.
  • Arc 739 shows no throw. My rule found no upward push in it at all, so I started its flight at its first frame. The header says every record starts before the ball leaves the hand. Either this one does not, or the push is too short to see. I have not checked which. It is also the arc where fitting gravity does worst (5.33 m/s², and 180.8 cm off at the hoop).
  • The margin depends on the 10 frames. With more of the flight to fit, the free model should do better. I have only tested 10 frames, and I have not checked how much of each flight your season records will show.

Assumptions. The coordinates have no unit in the header. I took them to be meters, since the rim height is given in meters. Gravity is 9.81 m/s², as you set it. Between release and the hoop the ball follows a parabola, so I ignored air drag and spin. The times are as recorded in the file.

Program. a5_which_model.py (attached) finds each flight, fits both models, prints a line for every arc and draws both figures.

What to notice

Bob asked which model, and the first sentence tells him. The second paragraph answers what each model is better at, with the residual and the error side by side. The memo picks one test, the height at the hoop, because that is probably what Bob will use the model for. The last bullet names the limit of the memo's own test (only 10 frames were tried).

A Technical Report

Example 2 · Technical report

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Summary

The model that holds gravity at 9.81 m/s² is the one to build on. Fitted to 10 frames of each flight and scored on the rest of the flight, its median rms height error was 3.7 cm against 13.6 cm for the model that fits gravity, over 23 training arcs. It was the closer of the two on 19 of them. The model that fits gravity always matches the frames it was fitted to a little more closely, and it narrows the gap as it is given more frames (7.1 against 3.9 cm at 14 frames). It does better the more of the flight it is given. For the part-flights tested here (6, 10 and 14 frames), holding gravity predicts better at every length.

Data

The training file holds 24 arcs of a basketball's position, tracked at 25 frames a second (one frame every 0.04 s). The header gives the vertical tracking noise as 3.07 cm. Each training record starts before the ball leaves the shooter's hand and ends 15 frames after the ball passes the rim. The three test files hold shots that start partway and stop before the hoop, which is presumably what the model will be given during the season. Counting frames after the shooter's last upward push gives a median of 7 frames in a short record, 11 in a mid record and 15 in a long one. The fits below use 6, 10 and 14 frames so that each matches one kind of record, with a frame to spare for finding the release.

Method

The two models of the ball's height z at time t are

  • Gravity fitted. z = c0 + c1t + c2t², with all three coefficients fitted, so the fitted gravity is −2c2.
  • Gravity held. z = c0 + c1t − ½gt² with g = 9.81 m/s². The known term is moved to the left side and z + ½gt² is fitted with a straight line, so this is still ordinary least squares.

Finding the flight. The vertical acceleration at each frame is estimated from a parabola through the 9 frames centered on it (0.32 s). Release is the first frame after the last upward reading at which the acceleration is within 5 m/s² of −9.81 and stays there for 4 frames in a row. The 9-frame estimate still wanders by several m/s² in mid-flight (Figure 1), which is why the tolerance is that wide. The flight ends at the frame before the acceleration leaves that band for 4 frames in a row (the ball has hit the rim, board or net), or before the ball drops below rim height (3.048 m), whichever comes first.

Testing. Each model is fitted to the first 6, 10 and 14 frames of each flight. The residual is the rms height gap on the fitted frames. The held-out error is the rms gap on all the remaining frames of the flight, which the fit did not see. An arc needs at least 3 frames left over to be scored, and all 23 arcs used have that at every length.

A Python program, a5_model_selection.py (listed in the appendix and submitted with this report), does all of the above and draws every figure.

The free model's residual was smaller than or equal to the fixed model's on all 23 arcs at every length, which least squares guarantees (it has one more coefficient to spend). Its held-out error was larger on most arcs at every length. Its fitted gravity ranges from −3.0 to 18.0 m/s² at 6 frames, from 5.9 to 12.3 at 10 and from 7.8 to 10.7 at 14. With a short stretch either curve can bend toward the noise, and the free model, with one more coefficient, likely bends further. Fitted to whole flights, the free model gives a median gravity of 9.61 m/s² (8.80 to 10.28 over 23 arcs). It is probably better suited to measuring gravity once the whole flight has been seen. Bob's season records stop before the hoop, so the model has to predict, and the fixed model predicts better at every length tested.

Errors and Anomalies

Arc 325 is filled in. Its height runs in straight lines from frame 25 to frame 44 (Figure 3). Measured heights carry about 3 cm of noise and never fall by an identical step frame after frame, so the tracker probably lost the ball near the top and the gap was filled. The arc was dropped, which leaves 23.

Uneven frame spacing. Inside the flights, 8 steps between frames are not 40 ms, ranging from 30 to 50 ms, in arcs 289, 1283, 83 and 739. The times were used as recorded. Whether the ball was really sampled at those times or the clock is rounded has not been checked.

Gravity a little low. The whole-flight median of 9.61 m/s² is under 9.81. The cause has not been found. Air drag, a small error in the tracker's height scale, or the flight windows themselves could each do it. The held-out errors above already include whatever effect it has on the fixed model.

Assumptions

  • The coordinates are in meters. The header gives no unit for them, but it gives the rim height in meters.
  • Gravity is 9.81 m/s², as Bob set it.
  • Between release and the rim the ball follows a parabola (no drag, no spin).
  • The flight runs from the release rule to the end rule above. A different rule would move the numbers. I have not tested whether it would change the ranking.
  • The number of frames after the last upward push in a test record stands for how much flight the model will be given during the season.

Appendix. Program

"""Assignment 5: model selection for the shot-flight model.

Run this from the unzipped pack folder. It compares two models of the ball's
height in free flight,

    free:   z = c0 + c1 t + c2 t^2           (c2 fitted, so gravity is fitted)
    fixed:  z = c0 + c1 t - (g/2) t^2        (g held at 9.81 m/s^2)

on every training arc. Each model is fitted to the first 6, 10 and 14 frames of
the flight and scored on the rest of the flight, which it did not see. It prints
the release frames, the frames of flight in the test records, a line per arc
and a summary table, and saves release.svg, errors_by_window.svg and arc_325.svg.
"""
import json

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

G = 9.81               # m/s^2
WINDOW = 9             # frames in each acceleration estimate (0.32 s)
TOL = 5.0              # m/s^2, how close to -g counts as "settled"
RUN = 4                # frames in a row it has to stay settled
RIM = 3.048            # m, rim height (from the header)
FIT_LENGTHS = [6, 10, 14]
MIN_HELD_OUT = 3       # an arc needs at least this many unseen frames to be scored


def load(name):
    with open(f'shots/{name}.json') as f:
        return json.load(f)


def accel(t, z):
    """Vertical acceleration at every frame, from a parabola through the 9 frames
    centered on it. NaN where the window runs off either end."""
    h = WINDOW // 2
    a = np.full(len(t), np.nan)
    for i in range(h, len(t) - h):
        c = np.polyfit(t[i - h:i + h + 1] - t[i], z[i - h:i + h + 1], 2)
        a[i] = 2 * c[0]
    return a


def last_push(t, z):
    """The last frame before the top where the acceleration is upward, meaning
    the hand is still on the ball. None if there is no such frame."""
    a = accel(t, z)
    top = int(np.argmax(z))
    last = None
    for i in range(top):
        if a[i] > 0:
            last = i
    return last


def release(t, z):
    """The first frame after the last upward push at which the acceleration is
    within TOL of -g and stays there for RUN frames. None if it never does."""
    a = accel(t, z)
    p = last_push(t, z)
    if p is not None:
        first = p + 1
    else:
        first = WINDOW // 2
    for i in range(first, len(t) - RUN + 1):
        if np.all(np.abs(a[i:i + RUN] + G) < TOL):
            return i
    return None


def flight_end(t, z):
    """Last frame of the flight: the frame before the acceleration leaves the
    band for RUN frames in a row (the ball has hit something), or the frame
    before the ball drops through rim height, whichever comes first."""
    a = accel(t, z)
    top = int(np.argmax(z))
    for i in range(top, len(t) - RUN + 1):
        if np.all(np.abs(a[i:i + RUN] + G) >= TOL) or z[i] < RIM:
            return i - 1
    return len(t) - 1


def straight_runs(z, frames=6):
    """First and last frame of any stretch where the height falls on a straight
    line for 'frames' steps or more. Measured heights carry about 3 cm of noise,
    so a straight line means the tracker filled a gap."""
    flat = np.abs(np.diff(z, 2)) < 0.001
    hits = []
    for i in range(len(flat) - frames + 2):
        if np.all(flat[i:i + frames - 1]):
            hits.append(i)
    if len(hits) == 0:
        return None
    return hits[0], hits[-1] + frames


def fit(t, z, fixed):
    if fixed:   # the known term moves to the left-hand side
        A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t])
        c = np.linalg.lstsq(A, z + 0.5 * G * t ** 2, rcond=None)[0]
        return np.array([c[0], c[1], -G / 2])
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t, t ** 2])
    return np.linalg.lstsq(A, z, rcond=None)[0]


def find_shot(shots, wanted):
    """The shot whose id is wanted."""
    for s in shots:
        if s['id'] == wanted:
            return s


def rms(c, t, z):
    return np.sqrt(np.mean((c[0] + c[1] * t + c[2] * t ** 2 - z) ** 2))


train = load('train')
shots = train['shots']
noise = train['header']['tracking_noise']['vertical_m']
print('vertical tracking noise from the header (m):', noise)

# how much flight does a test record give us to fit?
for name in ['test_short', 'test_mid', 'test_long']:
    seen = []
    for s in load(name)['shots']:
        t = np.array(s['t'])
        z = np.array(s['z'])
        seen.append(len(t) - 1 - last_push(t, z))
    print(f'{name:10}  frames after the last upward push: '
          f'median {np.median(seen):.0f}, range {min(seen)} to {max(seen)}')

# find the flight in every training arc
arcs = []
print()
print(f"{'arc':>5} {'release':>7} {'end':>4} {'frames':>6} {'steps not 40 ms':>16}")
odd_steps = []
for s in shots:
    t = np.array(s['t'])
    z = np.array(s['z'])
    gap = straight_runs(z)
    if gap:
        print(f"{s['id']:>5}  dropped: height is a straight line, "
              f"frames {gap[0]} to {gap[1]}")
        continue
    r = release(t, z)
    e = flight_end(t, z)
    steps = 1000 * np.diff(t[r:e + 1])     # ms between frames in the flight
    odd = steps[np.abs(steps - 40) > 5]
    odd_steps = odd_steps + list(odd)
    print(f"{s['id']:>5} {r:>7} {e:>4} {e - r + 1:>6} {len(odd):>16}")
    arcs.append((s['id'], t[r:e + 1], z[r:e + 1]))

print(f'steps other than 40 ms inside the flights: {len(odd_steps)}, '
      f'from {min(odd_steps):.0f} to {max(odd_steps):.0f} ms')

# both models, three fit lengths, scored on the unseen part of the flight
results = {}
for n_fit in FIT_LENGTHS:
    rows = []
    for arc_id, t, z in arcs:
        if len(t) - n_fit < MIN_HELD_OUT:
            continue
        tf = t[:n_fit]          # the fitted frames
        zf = z[:n_fit]
        th = t[n_fit:]          # the held-out frames
        zh = z[n_fit:]
        free = fit(tf, zf, False)
        fixed = fit(tf, zf, True)
        rows.append([rms(free, tf, zf), rms(fixed, tf, zf),
                     rms(free, th, zh), rms(fixed, th, zh), -2 * free[2]])
    # residuals and errors to cm, g stays in m/s^2
    results[n_fit] = np.array(rows) * [100, 100, 100, 100, 1]

print()
for n_fit, r in results.items():
    med = np.median(r, axis=0)
    print(f'{n_fit} frames fitted, {len(r)} arcs (medians over the arcs)')
    print(f'  residual (cm):     free {med[0]:.2f}  fixed {med[1]:.2f}  '
          f'free <= fixed on {np.sum(r[:, 0] <= r[:, 1])}')
    print(f'  held-out (cm):     free {med[2]:.1f}  fixed {med[3]:.1f}  '
          f'fixed closer on {np.sum(r[:, 3] < r[:, 2])}')
    print(f'  fitted g (m/s^2):  {med[4]:.2f}, '
          f'range {np.min(r[:, 4]):.1f} to {np.max(r[:, 4]):.1f}')

# whole flights, gravity fitted
g_whole = []
for arc_id, t, z in arcs:
    g_whole.append(-2 * fit(t, z, False)[2])
print()
print(f'g fitted to whole flights: median {np.median(g_whole):.2f}, '
      f'range {min(g_whole):.2f} to {max(g_whole):.2f} m/s^2, {len(g_whole)} arcs')

The speed climbs to almost 6 m/s at about 0.45 s while the hand is pushing, and then falls in a straight line. That straight line is gravity, which should take 9.81 × 0.04 = 0.39 m/s off the speed every frame. The jump at about 1.6 s is the ball hitting the rim. I am taking the release as the frame where the upward speed stops growing, found by walking back from the top of the arc. For the end, the rim is 0.23 m from the center of the hoop and the ball's radius is 0.12 m (both from the header), so the ball can touch the rim once its center is about 0.35 m from the hoop's center. I stop at 1 m to leave a few frames of margin.

G = 9.81

def release(t, z):
    # walk back from the top while the upward speed keeps growing;
    # the frame where it stops growing is where the hand let go
    vz = np.full(len(t), np.nan)
    vz[2:-2] = (z[4:] - z[:-4]) / (t[4:] - t[:-4])
    i = int(np.argmax(z))
    while i > 3 and (vz[i - 1] > vz[i] or vz[i - 2] > vz[i]):
        i = i - 1
    return i

def flight(s):
    t = np.array(s['t'])
    z = np.array(s['z'])
    dx = np.array(s['x']) - s['hoop_x']
    dy = np.array(s['y']) - s['hoop_y']
    end = int(np.argmax(np.hypot(dx, dy) < 1.0))   # first frame within 1 m
    r = release(t, z)
    return t[r:end], z[r:end], r, end

def fit_free(t, z):
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t, t**2])
    return np.linalg.lstsq(A, z, rcond=None)[0]

def fit_fixed(t, z):
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t])
    c = np.linalg.lstsq(A, z + 0.5*G*t**2, rcond=None)[0]
    return np.array([c[0], c[1], -G/2])

def rms_cm(c, t, z):
    return 100 * np.sqrt(np.mean((c[0] + c[1]*t + c[2]*t**2 - z)**2))

for s in train['shots']:
    t, z, r, end = flight(s)
    c = fit_free(t, z)    # the whole flight, gravity fitted
    print(f"{s['id']:5}  release {r:2}  1 m at {end:2}  frames {len(t):2}  "

On the half it was fitted to, the free model is closer on all 23 arcs, as it has to be with an extra coefficient. On the half it did not see, the fixed model is closer on 14 of 23, with a median of 4.2 cm against 5.8 cm. The free model's worst arc is 35.7 cm out and the fixed model's is 11.8 cm, so the free model's misses are the bigger ones. My answer for Bob is to build on the fixed model. The free model is better at matching the frames it has and at measuring gravity from a whole flight. The fixed model predicted the unseen frames better on 14 of 23 arcs, and that is the job Bob has for it. The difference with half a flight is smaller than I expected. With shorter pieces of flight I would expect the fixed model's lead to grow, although I have only tested halves.

What to notice

The notebook shows how each decision was reached (the speed plot for the release, the printed steps for arc 325). Each step can be checked against the output beneath it. Its test uses half of each flight, and it reports the smaller margin that test gives without stretching it. The flags and assumptions are gathered at the end so a reader does not have to find them in the middle.
