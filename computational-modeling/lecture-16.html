Solving a least-squares fit in one step

Least squares picks c0 and c1 to make S as small as possible.

The model is a straight line, y = c0 + c1x, with n measurements. These are a and b from lecture 13 with new names. S is the sum of squared residuals.

$$S(c_0, c_1) \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{n} \big(\, y_i - c_0 - c_1 x_i \,\big)^2$$

S is lowest where it stops sloping in both directions.

A line fitted to the shot’s distance from the hoop against time, from lecture 13. Each panel changes one parameter and holds the other at its best value.

At the minimum, both partial derivatives are zero.

∂S/∂c0 is the slope of the left panel on the last slide, found by treating c1 as a constant.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial S}{\partial c_0} &= -2\sum_i \big(y_i - c_0 - c_1 x_i\big) = 0 && \text{chain rule on each squared term} \\[6pt] \frac{\partial S}{\partial c_1} &= -2\sum_i x_i\big(y_i - c_0 - c_1 x_i\big) = 0 && \text{the inside's derivative is } {-x_i} \\[6pt] \sum_i \big(y_i - c_0 - c_1 x_i\big) = 0, &\quad \sum_i x_i\big(y_i - c_0 - c_1 x_i\big) = 0 && \text{divide both by } {-2} \end{aligned}$$

Rearranged, they become two linear equations, the normal equations.

Every entry is a sum over the data. In the first row, c0 added up n times gives n c0.

$$\begin{bmatrix} n & \sum x_i \\[2pt] \sum x_i & \sum x_i^2 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \textcolor{#8A6508}{c_0} \\[2pt] \textcolor{#8A6508}{c_1} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \sum y_i \\[2pt] \sum x_i y_i \end{bmatrix}$$

On the shot from lecture 13, c0 = 7.84 m and c1 = −6.22 m/s.

The fit uses 31 frames of distance against time, 0.04 s apart.

$$\begin{aligned} \begin{bmatrix} 31 & 18.6 \\ 18.6 & 15.128 \end{bmatrix}\!\begin{bmatrix} c_0 \\ c_1 \end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix} 127.24 \\ 51.651 \end{bmatrix} && \text{the sums} \\[6pt] 3.968\, c_1 &= -24.693 && \text{row 2} - 0.6 \times \text{row 1} \\[6pt] 31\, c_0 &= 127.24 - 18.6 \times (-6.223) && \text{back into row 1} \\[6pt] c_0 = \textcolor{#8A6508}{7.84}, \quad c_1 &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{-6.22} \end{aligned}$$

With p parameters the matrix is p × p, built from the same kind of sums.

Entry (j, k) is ∑ gj(x) gk(x) over the data, with the gs from lecture 15. A parabola uses 1, x and x².

$$\begin{bmatrix} n & \sum x & \sum x^2 \\ \sum x & \sum x^2 & \sum x^3 \\ \sum x^2 & \sum x^3 & \sum x^4 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} c_0 \\ c_1 \\ c_2 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \sum y \\ \sum xy \\ \sum x^2 y \end{bmatrix}$$

How many digits survive the solve

A computer keeps about 16 significant digits of every number.

This format is called a double. Anything past the sixteenth digit is rounded away.

$$\tfrac{1}{3} \;\to\; 0.3333333333333333 \qquad \tfrac{2}{3} \;\to\; 0.6666666666666666$$

In some systems a tiny change moves the answer a long way.

These are two nearly parallel lines. Changing one number by 0.0001 moves where they cross from (1, 1) to (0, 2).

$$\begin{aligned} u + v &= 2, \quad u + 1.0001\,v = 2.0001 && \text{solution } u = 1,\ v = 1 \\[8pt] u + v &= 2, \quad u + 1.0001\,v = \textcolor{#8A6508}{2.0002} && \text{solution } u = 0,\ v = 2 \end{aligned}$$

A condition number κ costs about log10 κ of the 16 digits.

κ (kappa) measures how much a small change in a system’s matrix moves its solution. For the system on the last slide κ is about 4×104, so about 4.6 digits are lost.

$$\text{digits left} \;\approx\; 16 - \log_{10} \kappa$$

On 21 points, degree 9 loses 13 of the 16 digits.

The points are evenly spaced from 0 to 1, with the normal equations built as before. Red bars leave 6 or fewer digits.

Forming A⊤A squares the condition number.

At degree 6, κ(A) = 1.8×104 and κ(A⊤A) = 3.4×108. The matrix of sums from earlier is A⊤A, where A is lecture 15’s table of columns. A library routine typically works with A itself.

$$\kappa\!\left(A^{\!\top}\!A\right) \;=\; \kappa(A)^{\textcolor{#8A6508}{2}}$$

What survives a badly conditioned solve

At degree 9 the two solves draw the same curve.

A sensor’s voltage was read at 21 temperatures from 0 to 100 °C. The red curve sits under the green one.

At degree 20 the solves differ by 7 V, and neither solve gives a warning.

With 21 parameters for 21 points, neither curve can be trusted between the points (residuals 1.76 and 0.95 mV).

The fitted curve is usually robust, and the coefficients are fragile.

At degree 9 the raw coefficients were off in about the fourth significant figure, while the curves agreed to 0.15 µV.
