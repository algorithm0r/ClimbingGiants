Example reports · Assignment 8

These are three reports for Assignment 8, Uncertainty Intervals, written in three different styles. All three describe the same example pack (crash test 9793, which no phrase assigns), so nothing here answers anybody's assignment. Each student reruns the driver's head injury calculation many times with noise added, and each chooses the noise and the level differently. Please see the Example reports page for what every report needs and who it is written for.

The three students agree on the value and on what the record shows. Their intervals differ in width by a factor of about six (0.15 against roughly 1), and each says which choice moved it. Each report is followed by a short note on what it does well. The purpose of the notes is to show that three styles can meet the same standard (plots identified at a glance, errors flagged, assumptions noted).

Things to watch for

Test 9793 starts 50 ms before impact, which gives 1000 quiet samples for measuring the noise. Many tests start 30 or 20 ms before impact, and at 10 kHz a 20 ms stretch holds only 200 samples. For this reason your noise estimate will probably be less certain, and a method that cuts pieces from that stretch (as the technical report does) has little to choose from. Set the piece length in samples from your own sample rate (the technical report's 200 samples is 10 ms only at 20 kHz).

The technical report's program is read one section at a time, beside a MATLAB version that prints the same results, on Reading the assignment 8 program.

A Memo

Example 1 · Memo

To: Alice

Re: How well we know the driver's head injury number

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

HIC15 for the driver is 150.4, with a 90% interval of 150.3 to 150.5. I would put my name beside both, as long as one sentence goes with them: the interval covers the noise in the head instruments and nothing else. (HIC15 is the head injury criterion searched over windows no longer than 15 ms. The regulation's limit for it is 700.)

What I varied. Before impact the car is towed at a steady speed, so the 50 ms of record before impact (1000 samples) shows what the head instruments read when nothing is happening. Their spread (standard deviation, sigma) is 0.811, 1.061 and 0.929 m/s² in the X, Y and Z directions, or 0.08 to 0.11 g (one g is 9.80665 m/s²). I added fresh random noise of that size to each channel and ran the whole calculation again. That includes the CFC 1000 smoothing filter the standard sets for head acceleration, the resultant (the three directions combined into one size) and the window search. I did this 1000 times. The interval runs from the 5th to the 95th percentile of the 1000 answers (the 5th percentile is the answer that 5% of the reruns fall below), so nine reruns in ten land inside it.

What I held fixed.

  • The filter class (CFC 1000) and the 15 ms window limit. The standards set both (SAE J211-1 and FMVSS 208).
  • The value of g (9.80665 m/s²).
  • No offset removed. The pre-impact means are small (−0.205 to 0.097 m/s²), and subtracting them moves HIC15 by +0.02.
  • The test itself. A second crash of the same car would probably give a different number, and the interval says nothing about that.

Two things need your attention before the meeting.

  • The interval is very narrow. It is 0.15 wide, about a tenth of a percent of the value. It says how much the instruments' noise moves the calculation, and the customer's engineers could easily read it as how well we know this car's HIC.
  • I assumed the noise is independent from one sample to the next. I have not checked this. If neighboring samples tend to move together, the noise would cancel less inside a 15 ms window, and the interval is probably too narrow.

Assumptions. Time zero in the file is the moment of impact, and the 50 ms before it is noise alone. The noise during the crash is the same size as before it. The three head channels (all in m/s², status ok) are calibrated correctly, since a scale error would move every rerun the same way and would not show in the interval.

Program. a8_hic_interval.py (attached) measures the noise, computes HIC15, runs the 1000 reruns and draws the figure.

What to notice

The first paragraph gives Alice the value, the interval, the level and the sentence she can say in the room. The figure puts the measured noise beside what it does to the answer. A reader sees what was varied and what came of it. The memo also names the one assumption it did not check (that the noise is independent), and that line is probably the most useful one in it.

A Technical Report

Example 2 · Technical report

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Summary

HIC15 for the driver of test 9793 is 150.4, with a 95% interval of 150.0 to 151.0. The interval comes from rerunning the whole calculation 1000 times with noise cut from the record's own pre-impact stretch, so it covers instrument noise and nothing else. The filter class and the window limit were held fixed by rule, and the value of g and the baseline by choice. Changing either choice leaves the answer inside the interval. I would put my name beside the value and the interval, provided the words "instrument noise only" go with them.

Data

The three driver head channels of test 9793 (codes 11HEADCG0000ACXP, ACYP and ACZP) are accelerations at the head's center of gravity in m/s². Each holds 7001 samples 0.05 ms apart (a sample rate of 20,000 per second), from −50 to +300 ms. Each header also gives a prefilter of 4000 Hz, which means the instrument smoothed the signal before it was recorded. The 1000 samples before impact are the noise record. Table 1 gives their mean and spread, and how much each sample resembles the samples after it (the autocorrelation at a given lag, which is 0 for noise that is independent from sample to sample and 1 for a copy).

Method

A Python program, a8_noise_intervals.py (listed in the appendix and submitted with this report), does every step below and draws every figure.

  1. The value. Each head channel is filtered at CFC 1000 (the four-pole Butterworth that SAE J211-1 assigns to head acceleration, run forward and then backward). The resultant is formed from the three filtered channels and divided by 9.80665 to put it in g. HIC is then searched over every window no longer than 15 ms (FMVSS 208), using a running total so that each window's integral is one subtraction.
  2. The noise model. Table 1 and Figure 2 show that the noise is far from independent, so I did not use independent Gaussian noise. For each rerun and each channel, I cut 10 ms pieces (200 samples) at random from the pre-impact record, with its mean removed, and joined them end to end to the length of the record. A piece keeps the noise's own wander inside it. I chose 10 ms because the X channel's autocorrelation has fallen to 0.02 there and the other two to about 0.2. Longer pieces would keep more of the wander, but the 50 ms record would then offer fewer different pieces to choose from.
  3. The reruns. Noise is added to all three raw channels and the whole of step 1 is run again, 1000 times. For comparison, the same is done with independent Gaussian noise of each channel's sigma (the "independent" model).
  4. The interval. The 95% interval runs from the 2.5th to the 97.5th percentile of the 1000 answers. I used percentiles because they make no assumption about the shape of the spread, and Figure 3 shows the spread is not centered on the value. I chose 95%, which the Reporting an Interval notes give as the common scientific default, because the number is going to a customer's engineers who will test it.
  5. The fixed choices. The two fixed choices that are judgment calls (the value of g and the baseline) are each changed on their own and HIC15 recomputed, to compare how far they move the answer with the interval's width.

HIC15 on the record is 150.39. Table 2 gives the intervals from both noise models at both levels.

With the noise cut from the record, the 95% interval is about six times wider than with independent noise (1.00 against 0.17). The independent noise mostly cancels inside a 15 ms window, and the record's own noise, which wanders over several milliseconds, does not. The block interval is also lopsided about the value, reaching 0.38 below it and 0.62 above, and its median (150.53) sits above the value. A likely reason is that the search keeps whichever window has the largest average, so noise that raises some window's average shows up in HIC15 more readily than noise that lowers it. I have not checked this.

Neither change moves HIC15 outside the block interval. Rounding g to 9.81 would, however, move the answer outside the independent-noise interval. The window limit and the filter class are set by the standards, so they are fixed by rule and not varied here.

Errors and Anomalies

The pre-impact noise is not independent from sample to sample. Neighboring samples have autocorrelations of 0.79 to 0.89 (Table 1). The 4000 Hz prefilter probably accounts for that much, since smoothing makes neighbors alike. It would not explain the Y and Z channels still being correlated at 5 ms (0.46 and 0.37), so something slower is in the record too.

The Y channel drifts. Across the 50 ms before impact it rises by a few m/s² and falls back (Figure 1), which is probably where its negative correlation at long lags comes from. It could be the dummy's head moving while the car is towed, or a slow drift in the instrument. I cannot tell which from the record. The block model treats it as noise, which probably makes the interval wider than the instrument noise alone would.

Assumptions

  • Time zero is the moment of impact, and the 50 ms before it holds no crash signal. The car is towed at constant speed, so the true acceleration there should be zero.
  • The noise during the crash has the same size and character as the noise before it. I have not checked this, and an instrument under the crash load may well behave differently.
  • The three channels' noise is independent of each other, so each gets its own draw. I have not checked this either.
  • The instruments are calibrated correctly. A scale error would move every rerun the same way, and no resampling can show it.

Appendix. Program

"""Assignment 8: an uncertainty interval on the driver's HIC15.

Run this from the unzipped pack folder. It
  1. measures the noise on the three driver head channels over the 50 ms
     before impact, including how much each sample resembles its neighbors,
  2. reruns the whole HIC15 calculation (CFC 1000 filter, resultant, 15 ms
     window search) 1000 times with noise added, for two noise models:
       independent: Gaussian noise drawn fresh for each sample, same sigma
       blocks:      10 ms pieces cut at random from the pre-impact record itself
  3. gives the 90% and 95% intervals for both,
  4. changes each judgment call that was held fixed (g and the baseline), one
     at a time, to see how far it moves HIC15.
Saves noise_before_impact.svg, noise_autocorrelation.svg and hic_draws.svg.
"""
import json
import math
import os

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

G = 9.80665        # standard gravity, m/s^2 in one g
DRAWS = 1000       # reruns per noise model
BLOCK = 200        # samples per block for the 'blocks' model (10 ms at 20 kHz)
CODES = ['11HEADCG0000ACXP', '11HEADCG0000ACYP', '11HEADCG0000ACZP']


def cfc(y, dt, cls):
    """SAE J211-1 four-pole Butterworth (Smoothing notes), forward then backward."""
    wd = 2 * math.pi * cls * 2.0775
    wa = math.tan(wd * dt / 2)
    d = 1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa + wa ** 2
    a0 = wa ** 2 / d
    b1 = -2 * (wa ** 2 - 1) / d
    b2 = (-1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa - wa ** 2) / d

    def one_pass(x):
        out = np.zeros(len(x))
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            out[i] = (a0 * x[i] + 2 * a0 * x[i - 1] + a0 * x[i - 2]
                      + b1 * out[i - 1] + b2 * out[i - 2])
        return out

    forward = one_pass(y)
    backward = one_pass(forward[::-1])
    return backward[::-1]


def hic(a, dt, limit_ms):
    """Largest HIC over every window up to limit_ms, a = resultant in g.
    Prefix sum of the trapezoid integral, one numpy pass per window length."""
    panels = (a[1:] + a[:-1]) / 2 * dt
    S = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(panels)])
    kmax = round(limit_ms / 1000 / dt)          # the limit in samples
    best = 0.0
    for k in range(1, kmax + 1):
        T = k * dt
        mean = np.clip((S[k:] - S[:-k]) / T, 0, None)   # every window of length k at once
        best = max(best, np.max(T * mean ** 2.5))
    return best


def pipeline(chs, dt, cls=1000, limit_ms=15, g=G):
    """The whole analysis: filter each component, resultant in g, HIC search.
    cls=None skips the filter."""
    if cls is not None:
        filtered = []
        for c in chs:
            filtered.append(cfc(c, dt, cls))
        chs = filtered
    total = 0
    for c in chs:
        total = total + c ** 2
    return hic(np.sqrt(total) / g, dt, limit_ms)


def autocorr(r, lag):
    """Correlation between the noise and itself shifted by lag samples."""
    return np.corrcoef(r[:-lag], r[lag:])[0, 1]


def independent_noise(pre, n, rng):
    return rng.normal(0, np.std(pre), n)


def block_noise(pre, n, rng):
    """Join random 10 ms pieces of the (mean-removed) pre-impact record."""
    r = pre - np.mean(pre)
    pieces = []
    for k in range(n // BLOCK + 1):
        s = rng.integers(0, len(r) - BLOCK + 1)     # a random start
        pieces.append(r[s:s + BLOCK])
    return np.concatenate(pieces)[:n]


name = os.listdir('crash')[0]           # the crash folder holds one file
with open(os.path.join('crash', name)) as f:
    test = json.load(f)
chans = []
for c in test['channels'].values():
    if c['code'] in CODES:
        chans.append(c)
dt = chans[0]['dt']
n = chans[0]['n']
t0 = chans[0]['t0']
t_ms = 1000 * (t0 + dt * np.arange(n))
before = t_ms < 0
raw = []                                # each channel's samples
pre = []                                # the part of each before impact
for c in chans:
    y = np.array(c['y'])
    raw.append(y)
    pre.append(y[before])

# 1. the noise
print(f"{len(chans)} head channels, {n} samples every {dt * 1000:g} ms, "
      f"{t_ms[0]:.2f} to {t_ms[-1]:.2f} ms")
print(f"pre-impact stretch: {np.sum(before)} samples, "
      f"{t_ms[0]:.2f} to {t_ms[before][-1]:.2f} ms")
LAGS = [1, 20, 100, 200]
columns = []
for lag in LAGS:
    columns.append(f'r({lag * dt * 1000:g} ms)')
print(f"{'code':18} {'prefilter':>9} {'mean':>7} {'sigma':>6} {'sigma':>7}   "
      + '  '.join(columns))
for m in range(len(chans)):
    c = chans[m]
    p = pre[m]
    r = p - np.mean(p)
    columns = []
    for lag in LAGS:
        columns.append(f'{autocorr(r, lag):9.2f}')
    print(f"{c['code']:18} {c['prefilter_hz']:>6} Hz {np.mean(p):7.3f} {np.std(p):6.3f} "
          f"{np.std(p) / G:6.4f}g   "
          + '  '.join(columns))

# 2. the value, then the reruns
value = pipeline(raw, dt)
print(f"\nHIC15 on the record: {value:.2f}")
rng = np.random.default_rng(310)
models = {'independent': independent_noise, 'blocks': block_noise}
draws = {}
for name, noise in models.items():      # noise is a function: one of the two above
    runs = []
    for k in range(DRAWS):
        noisy = []
        for m in range(len(raw)):
            noisy.append(raw[m] + noise(pre[m], n, rng))
        runs.append(pipeline(noisy, dt))
    draws[name] = np.array(runs)

# 3. the intervals
print(f"\n{'noise model':12} {'median':>7} {'90% interval':>17} {'width':>6} "
      f"{'95% interval':>17} {'width':>6}")
for name, d in draws.items():
    l90, h90, l95, h95 = np.percentile(d, [5, 95, 2.5, 97.5])
    print(f"{name:12} {np.median(d):7.2f} {l90:8.2f} to {h90:6.2f} {h90 - l90:6.2f} "
          f"{l95:8.2f} to {h95:6.2f} {h95 - l95:6.2f}")

# 4. what was held fixed, changed one at a time
l95, h95 = np.percentile(draws['blocks'], [2.5, 97.5])
print(f"\n{'held fixed, changed to':34} {'HIC15':>7} {'shift':>7} "
      f"{'x 95% width':>11}")
changes = {}
changes['g = 9.81'] = pipeline(raw, dt, g=9.81)
corrected = []
for m in range(len(raw)):
    corrected.append(raw[m] - np.mean(pre[m]))
changes['pre-impact mean subtracted'] = pipeline(corrected, dt)
for name, v in changes.items():
    ratio = abs(v - value) / (h95 - l95)
    print(f"{name:34} {v:7.2f} {v - value:+7.2f} {ratio:11.2f}")

# Figure 1: the pre-impact stretch of each channel
fig, axs = plt.subplots(3, 1, figsize=(8, 6), sharex=True)
for m in range(len(chans)):
    ax = axs[m]
    c = chans[m]
    ax.plot(t_ms[before], pre[m], linewidth=0.7)
    ax.set_title(f"{c['comment']}  [{c['code']}]", fontsize=9)
    ax.set_ylabel('acceleration (m/s$^2$)', fontsize=8)
axs[-1].set_xlabel('time from impact (ms)')
fig.suptitle('Driver head acceleration in the 50 ms before impact, test 9793',
             fontsize=10)
fig.tight_layout()

    corr = []
    for lag in lags:
        corr.append(autocorr(r, lag))
    plt.plot(lags * dt * 1000, corr, label=chans[m]['code'])
plt.axhline(0, color='gray', linewidth=0.8)

import math
G = 9.80665

def cfc(y, dt, cls):
    # SAE J211-1 filter, copied from the Smoothing notes
    wd = 2 * math.pi * cls * 2.0775
    wa = math.tan(wd * dt / 2)
    d = 1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa + wa ** 2
    a0 = wa ** 2 / d
    b1 = -2 * (wa ** 2 - 1) / d
    b2 = (-1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa - wa ** 2) / d
    def one_pass(x):
        out = np.zeros(len(x))
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            out[i] = (a0 * x[i] + 2 * a0 * x[i - 1] + a0 * x[i - 2]
                      + b1 * out[i - 1] + b2 * out[i - 2])
        return out
    forward = one_pass(y)
    backward = one_pass(forward[::-1])
    return backward[::-1]

def hic(a, dt, limit_ms=15):
    # prefix sum, then every window length up to the limit, all starts at once
    panels = (a[1:] + a[:-1]) / 2 * dt
    S = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(panels)])
    kmax = round(limit_ms / 1000 / dt)
    best = 0.0
    for k in range(1, kmax + 1):
        mean = np.clip((S[k:] - S[:-k]) / (k * dt), 0, None)
        best = max(best, np.max(k * dt * mean ** 2.5))
    return best

def head_hic(x, y, z, g=G, limit_ms=15):
    fx = cfc(x, dt, 1000)
    fy = cfc(y, dt, 1000)
    fz = cfc(z, dt, 1000)
    return hic(np.sqrt(fx ** 2 + fy ** 2 + fz ** 2) / g, dt, limit_ms)

value = head_hic(x, y, z)

plt.xlabel('time from impact (ms)')
plt.ylabel('head Y acceleration (m/s$^2$)')
plt.title('Driver head Y before impact, against independent noise of the same size')

They do not look alike. The independent noise jumps around from sample to sample. The recorded noise wanders up and down over several milliseconds, and across the whole 50 ms it climbs by a few m/s² and comes back. Slow wander like that will not cancel inside a 15 ms window, so I am flagging the independent-noise interval as probably too narrow. In place of a formula for the noise, I used the recorded noise itself. For each channel, I take the pre-impact stretch with its mean removed, repeat it end to end and start it at a random point. Every run then gets the real noise in its real order, wander and all. I am keeping 90%, since the Reporting an Interval notes give it as the usual level for engineering reports.

def real_noise(a):
    # the recorded noise before impact, mean removed, repeated end to end
    # and started at a random point
    r = a[quiet] - np.mean(a[quiet])
    s = rng.integers(len(r))
    return np.tile(r, n // len(r) + 2)[s:s + n]

What to notice

The notebook shows its first interval and then the check that overturned it, in the order the work was done. The plot of recorded against independent noise shows why a second noise model was needed. It is a good idea to put the answer at the top, as this one does, and to gather the flags and assumptions again at the end.
