State as a vector, for a belted occupant and a line of cars

Any system written as rates runs on the same loop: choose the state, write its rate function, keep the step line.

The first problem finds how far a belted occupant travels forward, relative to the car, when the car hits a barrier at 15.65 m/s.

Measured from the ground, the occupant is at xcar + u: the car’s position plus the travel relative to the car.

The belt stretches as the occupant moves forward, and as a spring it pulls back with a force k u.

$$F_{\text{spring}} = -k\,u,\qquad k = 296\ \text{kN/m}:\quad u = 0.1\ \text{m} \;\Rightarrow\; 29.6\ \text{kN}$$

The belt also soaks up energy, and as a damper it pulls back with a force c u̇, in proportion to how fast u changes.

$$F_{\text{damper}} = -c\,\dot u,\qquad c = 2.8\ \text{kN}\!\cdot\!\text{s/m}:\quad \dot u = 1\ \text{m/s} \;\Rightarrow\; 2.8\ \text{kN}$$

Newton’s second law from the ground: the mass times (acar + ü) equals the two belt forces.

$$m\,\frac{d^2}{dt^2}\big(x_{\text{car}} + u\big) = m\,\big(a_{\text{car}} + \ddot u\big) = \textcolor{#5F7038}{-k\,u} \;\textcolor{#8A6508}{-\;c\,\dot u}$$

Moved across, the car’s term makes this the damped spring you have solved, driven by −m acar.

$$m\,\ddot u + c\,\dot u + k\,u = \textcolor{#8A6508}{-m\,a_{\text{car}}(t)}$$

Left alone, this belt overshoots and swings back once every 105 ms, keeping 14% of its size after each full swing.

$$\omega_n = \sqrt{k/m} = 62.8\ \text{rad/s},\qquad \zeta = \frac{c}{2\sqrt{k\,m}} = 0.30,\qquad \omega_d = \omega_n\sqrt{1-\zeta^2} = 60.0\ \text{rad/s} \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{2\pi}{\omega_d} = 105\ \text{ms}$$

The car’s acceleration is a recording, 12,500 samples a second, so the forcing has no formula.

The occupant’s state is the travel u and how fast it changes, w, and the rate function reads the car’s acceleration at time t.

$$\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\begin{bmatrix} u \\ w \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} w \\ (-c\,w - k\,u)/m \;\textcolor{#8A6508}{-\; a_{\text{car}}(t)} \end{bmatrix}$$

A new rate function describes the occupant, and the step line is the ball’s.

The second problem puts 20 cars behind a car recorded on I-80 and finds how fast its slowdown travels back along the line.

In the model, a driver’s acceleration depends on three things: their speed v, the gap g, and how fast they are closing, Δv.

On an open road the model speeds up at up to amax and levels off at the desired speed vd.

$$\dot v_{\text{open road}} = a_{\max}\left[1 - \left(\frac{v}{v_d}\right)^{4}\right],\qquad a_{\max} = 1.27\ \text{m/s}^2,\quad v_d = 30.2\ \text{m/s}$$

Behind another car at the same speed, the model wants a gap of g0 when stopped plus T seconds of travel.

$$g^* = g_0 + v\,T,\qquad g_0 = 1.95\ \text{m},\quad T = 1.39\ \text{s}$$

Closing on the car ahead, the model wants extra room, v Δv / (2√(amaxb)), that grows with speed and closing rate.

$$g^* = g_0 + \max\!\left(0,\; v\,T + \frac{v\,\Delta v}{2\sqrt{a_{\max}\,b}}\right),\qquad b = 1.7\ \text{m/s}^2$$

Put together, the model’s acceleration is zero near the wanted gap, braking closer and speeding up farther back.

$$\dot v = a_{\max}\left[1 - \left(\frac{v}{v_d}\right)^{4} - \left(\frac{g^*}{g}\right)^{2}\right],\qquad g^* = g_0 + \max\!\left(0,\; v\,T + \frac{v\,\Delta v}{2\sqrt{a_{\max}\,b}}\right),\qquad \Delta v = v - v_{\text{ahead}}$$

The line’s state is 40 numbers, a position and a speed for each car, and each car’s acceleration depends on the car ahead.

In code, the cars ahead are the list of cars shifted by one, so one line gives all 20 accelerations.
