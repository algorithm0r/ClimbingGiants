Pólya's four steps can help turn a vague request into a problem you can solve.

Each step asks its own question, and each one applied to Assignment 1.

step 1Understand the problem What is the unknown, what are the data, and what is the condition? The unknown is what the pack holds. Alice and Bob set the conditions, and they do not ask for a judgment about injury.
step 2Devise a plan Have you seen a related problem, and can you use its method? The example reports and Anatomy of a signal are related problems. Your plan can be the questions you ask of each channel.
step 3Carry out the plan Can you see that each step is correct? Make sure you can read and explain each step your program takes, including the one that chooses what to plot.
step 4Look back Can you check the result, and can you use the result or the method for another problem? A chest compression of about 32 km fails that check. Every week you will reuse the code that reads your pack.

Six that came up, with their answers.

timeIs time zero the impact? Yes. Recording starts 20 to 50 ms before it, depending on the test.
seatsWhich seat is position 1, and which is 2? The code's second digit is the seat: 0 the vehicle, 1 the driver, 3 the front right passenger. The descriptions number the dummies, so the passenger is P2 with code 13.
unitsWhat unit is time in? Seconds, for the sampling interval and for the time of the first sample.
directionWhich way is +x? Forward. A car slowing against a barrier has a negative longitudinal acceleration.
locationThe location and description disagree Both describe one sensor. The location comes from the standard's fixed list, and the description is the lab's own words (a sill runs along the bottom of the door opening).
statusStatus says ok. Is the channel right? The status is the lab's verdict on the recording, and the chest displacement reads ok with the wrong unit.

Some of the strangest values in your reports have ordinary explanations.

Check a value against physics and units before calling it an error.

offsetBefore impact the average is not zero The true acceleration is close to zero, so what remains is the instrument's own offset and noise (week 3, Bias and noise).
spikesIs a sharp spike a bad reading? Count its samples. A spike over many samples is likely a real event, and a spike of one sample is likely the instrument. Labs sometimes mark spikes they doubt in the description, and our export kept only the last line of three of those notes (tests 8543, 8660 and 8993).
unitsNHTSA's files say 15.71 The original files store speed in m/s, and 15.71 m/s is 56.56 km/h. It is the same speed as the pack's 56.57, rounded.
timingThe head peaks long after the structure This is most likely the belt and airbag taking up the occupant's motion (next slide).

The driver's head starts to slow about 20 ms after the car does.

NHTSA test 10642 (2019 BMW X5), held back from the phrase assignment. A channel starts where its size first stays above 2 g for 1 ms.

from the car inwardThree channels The crossmember sensor responds about 3 ms after impact. The pelvis starts at about 18 ms and the head at about 26 ms, and the head's largest value comes at 79 ms. By 40 ms the car has slowed by about 7 m/s and the head by under 1 m/s.
whyThe belt, then the airbag The dummy carries on at about the car's original speed until the belt slack is taken up and the airbag fills, and then it usually slows over a shorter time than the car did. Some gap is expected (in this test, about 20 ms).

A cycle faster than half the sample rate shows up in the record as a slower one.

liveThree starting samples at one in 12
  • Each start draws a different wiggle on the crossmember, and none of them is the one that happened. The head barely changes.
  • A logger needs at least two samples in every cycle to see the cycle at all, and typically about ten to get its shape.
  • The slow record gives no sign that the cycle was faster.
  • The crossmember's change in velocity, the area under its curve, moves with the starting sample too (see the readout).

The same choice of rate comes up outside crash testing too.

beyond crash testsRecorded sound
CD audio samples at 44.1 kHz (green), more than twice the 20 kHz people can hear. At half that rate (red squares) an 18 kHz tone comes back as a 4 kHz one, so recorders filter out fast content before they sample (week 2, Aliasing).
beyond crash testsA design sweep
From the notes, a 2 Hz sweep finds 12.0 and passes a limit of 15. The structure reaches 25.0, between two grid points.
beyond crash testsRefining the sweep
Halving the step keeps moving the answer until the grid resolves the peak. A simulation can be rerun this way. A test record cannot.
