Computational Physical Modeling

Channel and quantity

One channel from a crash test is one quantity, recorded over time.

A quantity recorded over time is a time series.

All 394 channels in this test are one.

It came from an accelerometer, so these values should be accelerations.

Every channel carries a header of named fields that say what the values are.

Here is that header, as the file stores it.

{
  "Channel code": "11HEADCG0000ACXP",
  "Comments": "DRIVER HEAD X ACCEL PRIMARY",
  "Cut off frequency": "2000",
  "Dimension": "Acceleration",
  "Direction": "Longitudinal",
  "Location": "Head",
  "Number of samples": "4501",
  "Sampling interval": "8.0E-5",
  "Time of first sample": "-0.050000",
  "Unit": "m/(s*s)"
}

The header gives the dimension as Acceleration.

It gives the unit as meters per second squared.

Meters per second squared and g differ by a factor of 9.81.

The largest value here is 471 m/s², which is 48 g.

The header names six fields.

  • Dimension — what was measured
  • Location — what it was measured on
  • Direction — which axis
  • Unit — the scale the numbers are on
  • Sampling interval — how far apart they are in time
  • Time of first sample — when the recording starts

Longitudinal is the vehicle's fore-and-aft axis.

The other two are lateral, across the vehicle, and vertical.

Computational Physical Modeling

The time base

The file stores 4501 values and no times.

It stores two numbers instead.

t₀ = −0.050 s     Δt = 8.0×10⁻⁵ s

One over Δt is the sample rate.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{8.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{s}} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{12\,500\ \text{Hz}} \\[4pt] &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{12.5\ \text{kHz}} \end{aligned}$$

The record is 0.36 seconds long.

$$\begin{aligned} 4501\ \text{samples} &\rightarrow \textcolor{#8A6508}{4500\ \text{intervals}} \\[4pt] 4500 \times 8.0\times10^{-5}\ \text{s} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{0.36\ \text{s}} \\[4pt] -0.050\ \text{s} &\rightarrow \textcolor{#8A6508}{0.310\ \text{s}} \end{aligned}$$

Every sample's time is computed from those two.

$t_i = t_0 + i\,\Delta t$

Computed times cannot disagree with the values. A stored column of times can.

Plot the values on their own and the horizontal axis is sample number.

The largest value is at sample 1613, which is 79 milliseconds.

Δt wrong by a factor of two makes the velocity wrong by two and the displacement wrong by four.

Four channels from one test

Four channels from one test.

All four are longitudinal, every 80 µs, starting at −0.050 s.

They differ in quantity, body and unit.

Head and crossmember are both longitudinal acceleration in m/s².

The crossmember's largest value is at 10.8 ms. The head's is at 79 ms.

The belt and the airbag let the head keep moving after the structure has stopped.

A header that names the wrong unit

The chest deflection channel, same test.

Its unit field says meters. Its largest value is −32798.

At Meters, the chest deflects 32 kilometers.

$$\begin{aligned} 32798\ \text{m} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{32.8\ \text{km}} \\[4pt] 32798\ \text{mm} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{32.8\ \text{m}} \\[4pt] 32798\ \mu\text{m} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{32.8\ \text{mm}} \end{aligned}$$

The values are micrometers. The header says meters.
