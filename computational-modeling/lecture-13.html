Fitting a line to measured points

One shot was tracked 25 times a second for 1.2 s.

It is a missed three by Stephen Curry from November 2015, shown as its distance from the hoop and its height. These 31 frames were chosen as its free flight, up to the rim.

A straight line, y = a + bt, is set by two numbers, a and b.

A model is an equation the data should follow. Its shape is its form, and a and b are its parameters.

Fitting the line means finding the a and b that bring it closest to the points.

Each red stick is a residual, the data minus the line at that time.

The distance sits on a straight line, within 7 cm.

We chose a line because gravity pulls straight down, which leaves the horizontal motion steady. The right panel shows each residual in centimeters.

The RMS gives one typical size for all 31 residuals, 2.9 cm.

RMS stands for root mean square. The plain mean of the residuals is 0, so each one is squared first. Least squares is the fit that makes the RMS as small as possible.

$$\text{RMS} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{6.73^2 + 3.57^2 + 3.28^2 + \cdots}{31}} \;=\; \sqrt{\frac{257.6}{31}} \;=\; \sqrt{8.31} \;=\; 2.9\ \text{cm}$$

Both fitted numbers check out against the ball.

a is the line at t = 0, and b is its change per second.

$$\textcolor{#8A6508}{b} = -6.22\ \text{m/s} \qquad \text{the ball: } \frac{0.42 - 7.91}{1.2} \;=\; -6.24\ \text{m/s}$$

Now we choose a form we know is wrong, a straight line for the height.

The red sticks are the residuals. On the right, the height’s residuals show the distance’s pattern turned over, about 20 times larger.

On the height, neither number describes the ball.

The line is at 4.41 m at t = 0, more than a meter above the first frame, and its slope is nearly level.

The fitting was the same in both panels, and the straight line suited only the distance.

The RMS residual is 2.9 cm for the distance and 55 cm for the height. What form would follow the arc?

Computational Physical Modeling

A straight line for the vertical velocity

Differencing the heights turns the arc into vertical velocities.

Each velocity is the change in height between the frames either side, divided by the 0.08 s between them.

$$v_i \;=\; \frac{z_{i+1} - z_{i-1}}{t_{i+1} - t_{i-1}}$$

Gravity makes the vertical velocity a straight line.

Gravity changes the vertical velocity by the same amount every second, so the form is again a line, chosen from the physics.

$$v \;=\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{v_0} \;-\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{g}\,t \qquad \text{intercept } v_0,\ \text{slope } {-g}$$

The fitted slope is our measurement, g = 9.60 m/s².

Both v0 and g are fitted. The velocities’ RMS residual is 0.513 m/s.

Our measurement is 2.1% below the known 9.81 m/s².

Physics gives g = 9.81 m/s² near the earth’s surface, so we can check how far off our measurement is.

$$\frac{9.81 - 9.60}{9.81} \;=\; \frac{0.21}{9.81} \;\approx\; 0.021 \;=\; 2.1\%$$

A number we already know does not have to be fitted.

Holding g at 9.81 leaves one parameter, v0, for the data to find.

$$\begin{aligned} v &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{v_0} - \textcolor{#8A6508}{g}\,t && \text{two parameters, } g \text{ fitted} \\[8pt] v &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{v_0} - 9.81\,t && \text{one parameter, } g \text{ held} \end{aligned}$$

Holding g at 9.81 moves −gt to the data side.

v + 9.81t should be the same number at every frame, and the fit takes its average as v0.

$$\begin{aligned} v &= v_0 - g\,t && \text{the model} \\[8pt] v + g\,t &= v_0 && \text{add } g\,t \text{ to both sides} \\[8pt] \underbrace{v + 9.81\,t}_{\text{data}} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{v_0} && \text{one parameter left to fit} \end{aligned}$$

With g held, the RMS residual moves from 0.513 to 0.517 m/s.

The dashed line is the fit with g free. Holding g costs 0.004 m/s of residual for a g we trust.

Finding the free flight in a tracked shot

Where free flight starts and ends is a choice we make.

Nothing in the tracking marks the release or the arrival, so we pick a rule for each.

We take the release as the frame where the vertical velocity stops growing.

Each velocity is the change in height from two frames before to two frames after, over 0.16 s. After the release, gravity should make it fall about 0.39 m/s a frame.

Step back from the top of the flight while an earlier velocity is higher.

Looking two frames back keeps one noisy frame from stopping the search. On shot 1092 it stops at frame 11.

The window rests on three choices, so each is stated with the result.

On shot 1092 they give frames 11 to 36, which is 26 frames. Different choices would give a different window and a slightly different g.

Choosing the form of a model

Each form has its own parameters, and the fit finds those.

The parameters are in gold. Everything else in each form is fixed before the fit.

$$\begin{aligned} y &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{a} + \textcolor{#8A6508}{b}\,t && \text{a straight line} \\[6pt] v &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{v_0} - 9.81\,t && \text{the velocity, with } g \text{ held} \\[6pt] z &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{z_0} + \textcolor{#8A6508}{v_0}\,t - \tfrac{1}{2}\,\textcolor{#8A6508}{g}\,t^2 && \text{the height, a parabola} \\[6pt] y &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{a}\,e^{-\textcolor{#8A6508}{k}\,x} && \text{a decay} \end{aligned}$$

We choose the form by asking what we already know about the data.

Three kinds of knowledge supply most forms. They are listed by how much the fitted numbers mean.

firstThe physics A projectile’s parabola has a height, a vertical velocity and an acceleration as its parameters.
secondWhat y does for large x Decay to zero suggests ae−kx, and leveling off suggests ax/(b + x).
thirdConvenience A polynomial fits, though its coefficients mean little one at a time.

Adding parameters to a fit

Each extra degree adds one more parameter for the fit to adjust.

Ten points from a known parabola plus noise, fitted at degree 1, 2 and 3, with 2, 3 and 4 parameters. Each title gives the RMS of the residuals.

More parameters never fit the points worse, and they can still give a worse answer.

Degree 9 has ten parameters for ten points, so it passes through every one. The dashed curve is the parabola the points came from.

The residual falls to zero while the error climbs back.

The error is the RMS gap to the true curve. On real data the true curve is unknown, so we built these points to watch the error change.
