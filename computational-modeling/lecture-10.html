Noise in a derivative of sampled data

About 3 cm of tracking noise gave about 0.5 m/s of scatter in the speed.

Differentiate again and the scatter is as large as gravity.

Computational Physical Modeling

Where the amplification comes from

A central difference splits into a signal part and a noise part.

$$y_i = s_i + \varepsilon_i$$
$$\frac{y_{i+1} - y_{i-1}}{2h} \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{s_{i+1} - s_{i-1}}{2h}}_{\text{signal}} \;+\; \underbrace{\textcolor{#8A6508}{\frac{\varepsilon_{i+1} - \varepsilon_{i-1}}{2h}}}_{\text{noise}}$$

Two independent noises, each of size $\sigma$, differ by about $\sigma\sqrt{2}$.

$$\operatorname{sd}\!\left(\varepsilon_{i+1} - \varepsilon_{i-1}\right) \;=\; \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \sigma^2} \;=\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{\sigma\sqrt{2}}$$

Dividing by $2h$ leaves noise of $\sigma/(h\sqrt{2})$ in the derivative.

$$\frac{\sigma\sqrt{2}}{2h} \;=\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{\frac{\sigma}{h\sqrt{2}}}$$

The amplification factor $1/(h\sqrt{2})$ depends only on the sample spacing.

The same factor applies to any signal and any instrument.

25 samples a secondThe ball, about 18 With $h = 0.04$ s, each meter of height noise becomes about 18 m/s of speed noise.
12,500 samples a secondThe crash record, about 8,800 With $h = 0.08$ ms, the accelerometer’s noise is multiplied about 8,800 times.
same instrumentSampling 10 times faster Multiplies the noise in the derivative by about 10, if the noise stays independent.

The same misreading over a shorter baseline gives a larger slope error.

Checking the formula on a crash record

Before impact the true jerk is zero, so all of this is noise.

The derivative of acceleration is jerk, in m/s³. Here its noise is 16,507 m/s³.

The formula predicts 21,012 m/s³, so the measured noise is 79% of the prediction.

$$\frac{\sigma}{h\sqrt{2}} \;=\; \frac{2.377}{(8\times10^{-5})(1.414)} \;=\; 21{,}012 \qquad\qquad \frac{16{,}507}{21{,}012} \;=\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{0.79}$$

The derivation assumed neighboring noise values are independent.

If neighbors move together, subtracting them cancels more of the noise.

$$\operatorname{sd}\!\left(\varepsilon_{i+1} - \varepsilon_{i-1}\right) \;=\; \sqrt{\sigma^2 + \sigma^2} \qquad \textcolor{#8A6508}{\text{only if the two are independent}}$$

Correlation measures how closely one sample follows another.

Each point pairs a sample with the one k later. ρ (rho) measures the correlation.

The two samples a central difference uses have a correlation of 0.38.

With the correlation included, the prediction is 16,517 m/s³, within 0.1%.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{predicted} &= \frac{\sigma\sqrt{2(1-\rho)}}{2h} \;=\; \frac{2.377\sqrt{2(1-0.382)}}{2\,(8\times10^{-5})} \;=\; 16{,}517\\[0.8em] \text{measured} &= 16{,}507\\[0.8em] \text{difference} &= \frac{10}{16{,}507} \;=\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{0.06\%} \end{aligned}$$

Computational Physical Modeling

Integration and differentiation compared

Integration and differentiation are vulnerable to different errors.

adds neighborsIntegration Then multiplies by $h$. Noise summed over $n$ samples grows only as $\sqrt{n}$. A constant offset grows without limit.
subtracts neighborsDifferentiation Then divides by $h$. Noise grows by $\sqrt{2}$ in the difference, then by $1/h$. A constant offset is removed entirely.

The spacing is fixed, so reduce the noise before differencing.

Both ways give up some resolution in time.
