What “best” means in a line of best fit

A line through the points leaves a residual at every point.

A residual is the gap between the measurement and the model, drawn here as red sticks.

Two lines can each be closer at some points and farther at others.

To say which line is better, we need one score for all ten residuals.

Three common scores turn the residuals into one number.

These scores are called norms, and $r_i$ is the residual at point $i$. Norm 2 is the standard because squaring lets the best line come from two linear equations.

add the sizesNorm 1 $\|r\|_1 = \sum_i |r_i|$ A residual twice as big counts twice as much.
add the squaresNorm 2 $\|r\|_2 = \sqrt{\textstyle\sum_i r_i^2}$ A residual twice as big counts four times as much. Least squares minimizes $\sum_i r_i^2$.
take the largestNorm ∞ $\|r\|_\infty = \max_i |r_i|$ Only the largest residual counts.

On residuals of 3 and 1, the 3 makes up 75%, 90% and 100% of the scores.

Each panel shows what the 3 and the 1 add to one score.

All three scores belong to one family.

This is the p-norm. ri is the residual at point i, and there are m points.

$$\|r\|_p \;=\; \left( \sum_{i=1}^{m} |r_i|^{\textcolor{#8A6508}{p}} \right)^{1/\textcolor{#8A6508}{p}}$$

Three norms fitted to data with one bad point

Ten points lie near y = 2 + 1.5x, with one moved up by 6.

The data are constructed, so the true line is known.

On the same ten points, the three norms give three different lines.

The dashed line is the true line, y = 2 + 1.5x. Least squares tilts toward the bad point, and norm ∞ lifts the whole line.

After squaring, the bad point makes up 86% of the sum least squares minimizes.

For these data norm 1 is best, since the bad point does not move its line.

Each panel refits its norm without the point at x = 6 (dashed). The title gives how far the line moved, at most, over the data.

Choosing a norm for a fit

Each norm works best for one kind of residual.

In practice norm 2 is the default, and the scatter is usually assumed normal to match it.

normal scatterNorm 2 It works best when the scatter is symmetric and bell-shaped, with no bad points.
bad points expectedNorm 1 A bad point counts in proportion to its size.
a tolerance everywhereNorm ∞ It states a tolerance like “every point within 0.02” directly.

A tolerance on every point is checked with the largest residual.

For a requirement like “within 0.02 of every point”, check the largest residual whatever norm the fit used.

A report names the norm, gives the fit, and says why.

This is one way to report the fits from this video.

ten points, y = a + bxSlope 1.502 and intercept 2.120, fitted by norm 1 Least squares gives a slope of 1.614. The difference comes from the point at x = 6, whose squared residual, 5.24², is 86% of the least-squares sum of squares. Without that point, least squares gives a slope of 1.505 and the norm 1 fit does not change. We report the norm 1 fit and flag the point at x = 6 as a likely error.
