Example reports

Example reports · Assignment 6

These are three reports for Assignment 6, The Rim Crossing, written in three different styles. All three use the 12 short records and the training arcs in the example pack (test 9793, which no phrase assigns). Nothing here answers anybody's assignment. The three students made different choices about which frames to fit, how to find the root and how to decide that a shot is too close to call, and each one says why. Please see the Example reports page for what every report needs and who it is written for.

Each report is followed by a short note on what it does well. The purpose of the notes is to show that different methods can meet the same standard. That is, plots are identified at a glance, errors are flagged and assumptions are noted. The three reports do not call every shot the same way, because their frames and their tests differ (and on these records a frame more or less often moves a prediction a long way). Every one of their results and figures comes from the program that is attached, and the few other numbers come from the pack's header or the writer's own assignment 5 report.

The technical report's program is read one section at a time, beside a MATLAB version that prints the same results, on Reading the assignment 6 program.

Before trusting the calls I made the same calls on the 23 usable training arcs, cut to the same number of frames, and compared them with the play-by-play. All 3 arcs I called in were made. How I decided. The record does not say when the ball leaves the hand, so I used my rule from assignment 5. The vertical acceleration comes from a parabola through the 9 frames around each frame, and the last frame where it is upward is the last push of the hand. In assignment 5 I started the flight 5 frames after that push. On these records that would leave only 2 to 6 frames, so I started 2 frames after it, which leaves 5 to 9. I held gravity at 9.81 m/s², as I recommended in assignment 5. Fitting it to these few frames instead gives values from 4.6 to 21.9 m/s², which suggests those fits mostly follow the noise. Across the floor I fitted a straight line to each coordinate. The quadratic formula then gives both times the fitted ball is at rim height, and the call comes from where it is at the later one.

How I know it is the right crossing. The earlier of the two roots falls between 0.66 and 0.75 s on every record, and each record's own frames first reach rim height within 0.04 s of it (Figure 1 shows one). That is within a few frames of the last push, while the ball is still leaving the shooter's hands, so the earlier root is the ball going up. The later root comes after the top of the fitted flight, with the ball falling, and it is the one in the table.

Three things need your attention.

  • The records start earlier than the header says. The header says the short records begin "partway into the flight". They begin with the ball about 1 m above the floor in the shooter's hands (Figure 1), and the last upward push comes at frame 15 to 19. Only the last few frames are flight, and every number above rests on them.
  • Three calls sit close to a line. Shot 332 is 12 mm inside the in radius, 1317 is about 4 cm outside the out radius and 1326 is about 5 cm inside the in radius. A frame more or less in the fit could likely move any of them across.
  • The lines assume the ball comes straight down. The in radius is the hoop's radius less the ball's, and the out radius is the two added, both from the header. A real shot arrives at an angle, so the lines sort shots only roughly. That may be part of why 3 of the training arcs I called out were made, though I have not checked it.

Assumptions. The coordinates are in meters (the header gives the rim and both radii in meters and the coordinates no unit). Gravity is 9.81 m/s². In flight the ball's height is a parabola and each floor coordinate a straight line, so I ignored air drag and spin. The training arcs were cut 7 frames after the last push, which is about what the short records hold. The times are as recorded.

Program. a6_rim_call.py (attached) finds each flight, fits it, finds both roots, makes the calls on the short records and the training arcs, and draws both figures.

A Technical Report

Example 2 · Technical report

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Summary

On this pack, the time of the crossing can be predicted from the short records, and the place cannot be predicted well enough to call a shot. The 12 crossings come 0.74 to 1.06 s after the last frame of their records. On 23 training arcs cut to the same 7 frames of flight, the predicted time was within 0.020 s of the time the whole flight gives on the median arc, and 0.082 s off at worst. The predicted place was 0.232 m from the whole flight's on the median arc and 0.806 m at worst. That median is twice the in radius (0.109 m) and about the width of the rim band (0.239 m). Narrowing the in and out bands by it leaves 11 of the 12 records too close to call and calls 685 out. On the training arcs the same rule called 3 arcs out, and all 3 were made, so the call for 685 is probably not one to act on.

Data

The short-record file holds 12 shots tracked at 25 frames a second. Its header says the records begin "partway into the flight" and end before the ball reaches the hoop. They begin earlier than that. Each starts with the ball 0.58 to 1.43 m above the floor, and the last frame with an upward acceleration is frame 15 to 19 (Figure 1), so most of each record appears to be the ball in the shooter's hands. Only the 6 to 10 frames after the last upward push are free flight (7 on the median record).

The header gives the hoop's inner radius (0.2286 m) and the ball's radius (0.1195 m). Treating the ball as coming straight down, a crossing under 0.1091 m from the hoop center clears the rim, one over 0.3481 m cannot touch it, and one between touches the rim. The training file holds 24 whole arcs with the play-by-play result. Arc 325 has a filled-in stretch (found in assignment 5) and is left out, which leaves 23.

Method

The flight. The release rule from assignment 5 looks for four acceleration estimates in a row near −9.81 m/s². Each estimate needs 4 frames on either side, so on a short record they stop a few frames after the last push and the rule has almost nothing to test. I kept its first step instead. The last frame before the top with an upward acceleration is the last push, and the flight is every frame after it.

The model. The height is z = c0 + c1t − ½gt² with g held at 9.81 m/s², fitted by least squares as in assignment 5. Each floor coordinate, measured from the hoop center, is a straight line in t.

The root. The crossing is a root of f(t) = z(t) − 3.048. Bisection stays inside its bracket, and a bracket that starts at the top of the fitted parabola can hold only the falling root, so I used bisection on f, from the top (t = −c1/2c2) to 2 s after it, until the bracket was 10−6 s wide. That takes 23 evaluations of f on every record. At the top f is positive and 2 s later it is negative, and a downward parabola falls the whole time after its top, so the one root in the bracket is the falling crossing. The parabola's other root comes before the top, while the ball is rising. As a check, the program also solves the quadratic with numpy's roots and stops if its later root differs from the bisection's by more than the tolerance. None did.

The training check. Each training arc was cut to 7 frames after its last push (the median for the short records), and the method above was run on it. The prediction was compared with the same model fitted to the whole flight, from the frame after the push to the last frame at least 1 m from the hoop across the floor. I did not use the crossing read off the frames as the reference. On 10 of the 24 training arcs the height rises again between the last frame 1 m out and the first frame under the rim, so the ball likely hit the rim or the backboard first, and the crossing in the frames comes after the bounce, somewhere else. A fit that stops 1 m out ends before the ball can reach the rim (the out radius is 0.348 m). It is itself a fit, though it reaches only the last meter or so past its frames, so the difference likely measures what is lost by having 7 frames. The time error is the difference between the two crossing times, and the place error is the distance between the two crossing points. The same cut arcs were also fitted with gravity free. The calls on the cut arcs were compared with the play-by-play twice, once with the in and out bands as the header gives them and once with each band narrowed by the median place error (a margin of 0.232 m).

A Python program, a6_rim_crossing.py (listed in the appendix and submitted with this report), does all of the above and draws every figure.

The calls on the 23 cut arcs against the play-by-play. With the bands as the header gives them, 11 arcs were called and 8 of those calls were right (both in calls, and 6 of the 9 out calls). With the margin, the 3 arcs called out were all made. Two of them, 83 and 1144, had the largest place errors in the set (0.806 and 0.525 m). On these arcs a prediction far from the hoop was more likely a poor fit than a clear miss.

The time holds up and the place does not. With gravity held, the time of the crossing depends on the height and upward speed the frames give, and the time errors suggest 7 frames fix both well enough. The place depends on the floor speed from a straight line through 7 frames, each with about 3 cm of tracking noise according to the header, carried about a second ahead. A small error in that speed likely becomes a few tenths of a meter at the hoop, which is the size of the place errors found.

Errors and Anomalies

The records start in the hand. The header says the short records begin partway into the flight. Each begins with the ball 0.58 to 1.43 m up, the hand is still pushing it at frame 15 to 19, and its heights pass rim height on the way up at frame 17 to 19, close to the last push. A program that took the header at its word and fitted every frame would fit the throw.

Uneven steps in two records. Record 685 has one step between fitted frames that is not 40 ms, and 503 has two. The times were used as recorded. Record 685 is also the one call the margin rule makes, at 0.993 m, with only 6 frames fitted. The uneven step may have moved it, though I have not checked.

The training records end differently from their header. The header says each training record ends 15 frames past the rim crossing. Counting from the first frame under the rim after the top, arcs 325, 1095 and 1307 end after 8, 6 and 11 frames. In addition, the ball rises again before it reaches rim height on 10 arcs (1092, 1223, 1111, 1095, 700, 1039, 739, 1151, 749 and 372), so on those the first frame under the rim is not where free flight crossed it. The training check uses no frame within 1 m of the hoop, and the play-by-play result does not depend on the frames, so neither problem reaches the numbers above.

Arc 739 has no push. No frame before the top of this training arc has an upward acceleration, so its flight was started at frame 4, the first frame with an estimate.

Assumptions

  • The coordinates are in meters. The header gives no unit for them, but it gives the rim height and both radii in meters.
  • Gravity is 9.81 m/s². In flight the height is a parabola and each floor coordinate a straight line (no drag, no spin).
  • The flight starts at the frame after the last upward push. The training arcs were cut 7 frames after it, and the short records hold 6 to 10.
  • The whole flight's crossing, from a fit that stops 1 m from the hoop, stands in for the true one in the training check. It is a prediction too, so the check measures the cost of having few frames, not the model's own error.
  • The in and out radii treat the ball as coming straight down.

Appendix. Program

"""Assignment 6: when and where each short-record shot comes down through the rim.

Run this from the unzipped pack folder. For each of the 12 short test records it
finds the frames of free flight, fits the height with gravity held at 9.81 m/s^2
and the two floor coordinates with straight lines, and finds the falling crossing
of rim height by bisection. Before that it tries the same method on the training
arcs, cut to the same number of flight frames, and compares each prediction with
the one the whole flight gives and with the play-by-play. It prints what it finds
and saves short_record.svg, training_check.svg and predictions.svg.
"""
import json

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

G = 9.81               # m/s^2, held (assignment 5)
WINDOW = 9             # frames in each acceleration estimate, as in assignment 5
NEAR_HOOP = 1.0        # m, a whole flight ends at the last frame this far from the hoop
TOL_T = 1e-6           # s, bisection stops when the bracket is this narrow
SKIP = [325]           # training arc with a filled-in gap (found in assignment 5)
SHOW = 158             # the short record drawn in short_record.svg


def load(name):
    with open(f'shots/{name}.json') as f:
        return json.load(f)


def accel(t, z):
    """Vertical acceleration at every frame, from a parabola through the 9 frames
    centered on it. NaN where the window runs off either end."""
    h = WINDOW // 2
    a = np.full(len(t), np.nan)
    for i in range(h, len(t) - h):
        c = np.polyfit(t[i - h:i + h + 1] - t[i], z[i - h:i + h + 1], 2)
        a[i] = 2 * c[0]
    return a


def last_push(t, z):
    """The last frame before the top where the acceleration is upward, meaning
    the hand is still on the ball."""
    a = accel(t, z)
    top = int(np.argmax(z))
    last = None
    for i in range(top):
        if a[i] > 0:
            last = i
    return last


def fit_height(t, z, held):
    """Parabola z = c0 + c1 t + c2 t^2, with c2 = -g/2 when gravity is held."""
    if held:
        A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t])
        c = np.linalg.lstsq(A, z + 0.5 * G * t ** 2, rcond=None)[0]
        return np.array([c[0], c[1], -G / 2])
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t, t ** 2])
    return np.linalg.lstsq(A, z, rcond=None)[0]


def fit_line(t, v):
    """Straight line v = b0 + b1 t (no drag, so the floor speed is constant)."""
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t])
    return np.linalg.lstsq(A, v, rcond=None)[0]


def above_rim(c, t, rim):
    """f(t): how far the fitted ball is above rim height at time t."""
    return c[0] + c[1] * t + c[2] * t ** 2 - rim


def falling_crossing(c, rim):
    """Bisection on f between the top of the parabola and 2 s after it. The
    parabola is above the rim at its top and only falls after it, so the one
    root in that bracket is the falling crossing. Returns the root and the
    number of evaluations of f."""
    a = -c[1] / (2 * c[2])          # time of the top
    b = a + 2.0
    fa = above_rim(c, a, rim)
    fb = above_rim(c, b, rim)
    if fa <= 0 or fb >= 0:
        return None, 2              # the fitted ball never gets above the rim
    evals = 2
    while b - a > TOL_T:
        m = (a + b) / 2
        fm = above_rim(c, m, rim)
        evals = evals + 1
        if fm > 0:
            a = m                   # still above the rim, so the root is later
        else:
            b = m
    return (a + b) / 2, evals


def predict(s, first, last, rim, held=True):
    """Fit frames first..last of shot s and return the crossing time, where the
    ball is then (x and y from the hoop center, m) and the evaluations used."""
    t = np.array(s['t'][first:last + 1])
    c = fit_height(t, np.array(s['z'][first:last + 1]), held)
    tc, evals = falling_crossing(c, rim)
    if tc is None:
        return None
    bx = fit_line(t, np.array(s['x'][first:last + 1]) - s['hoop_x'])
    by = fit_line(t, np.array(s['y'][first:last + 1]) - s['hoop_y'])
    return tc, bx[0] + bx[1] * tc, by[0] + by[1] * tc, evals


def call(d, in_r, out_r, margin):
    """In, out or too close, with the bands narrowed by the margin."""
    if d + margin < in_r:
        return 'in'
    if d - margin > out_r:
        return 'out'
    return 'too close'


def near_hoop_end(s):
    """Last frame at least NEAR_HOOP from the hoop, measured across the floor."""
    dx = np.array(s['x']) - s['hoop_x']
    dy = np.array(s['y']) - s['hoop_y']
    return int(np.argmax(np.hypot(dx, dy) < NEAR_HOOP)) - 1

My plan is to find the flight in each record, fit it with gravity held, find the falling root with Newton's method, and then see how far each prediction moves when its last frame is left out. def release(t, z):
    # assignment 5's rule: walk back while the upward speed keeps growing.
    # These records stop while the ball is still rising, so the walk starts
    # at the last frame that has a speed, not at the top.
    vz = np.full(len(t), np.nan)
    vz[2:-2] = (z[4:] - z[:-4]) / (t[4:] - t[:-4])
    i = len(t) - 3
    while i > 3 and (vz[i - 1] > vz[i] or vz[i - 2] > vz[i]):
        i = i - 1
    return i

Fitting gravity to so few frames gives values from 0.41 to 13.52 m/s². A ball in free flight falls at about 9.8, so these fits are probably following the noise, and I am holding gravity at 9.81, as I recommended to Bob in assignment 5. With gravity held, the height above the rim is f(t) = c0 + c1t − 4.905t² − 3.048, and its derivative is f′(t) = c1 − 9.81t, which Newton's method needs. The parabola crosses rim height twice, so I start Newton half a second after the top of the fitted flight and check which way f is going at the root. First I try both sides on record 158.

G = 9.81

def fit_held(t, z):
    # z + (g/2) t^2 = c0 + c1 t, a straight line
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t])
    return np.linalg.lstsq(A, z + 0.5*G*t**2, rcond=None)[0]

def f(c, t):
    # how far the fitted ball is above the rim at time t
    return c[0] + c[1]*t - 0.5*G*t**2 - RIM

def newton(c, t):
    for k in range(1, 51):
        t = t - f(c, t) / (c[1] - G*t)     # f'(t) = c1 - g t
        if abs(f(c, t)) < 1e-10:
            return t, k
    return None, k

def predict(s, first, last):
    t = np.array(s['t'][first:last])
    c = fit_held(t, np.array(s['z'][first:last]))
    top = c[1] / G                     # f'(t) = 0 at the top
    tc, steps = newton(c, top + 0.5)   # start on the falling side
    # which way is f going at the root?
    if f(c, tc - 1e-4) > 0 and f(c, tc + 1e-4) < 0:
        way = 'falling'
    else:
        way = 'not falling'
    A = np.column_stack([np.ones_like(t), t])
    bx = np.linalg.lstsq(A, np.array(s['x'][first:last]) - s['hoop_x'], rcond=None)[0]
    by = np.linalg.lstsq(A, np.array(s['y'][first:last]) - s['hoop_y'], rcond=None)[0]
    return tc, bx[0] + bx[1]*tc, by[0] + by[1]*tc, steps, way
