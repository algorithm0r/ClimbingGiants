Example reports · Assignment 3

These are three reports for Assignment 3, Impact Speed and Crush Distance, written in three different styles. All three describe the same example pack (crash test 9793, which no phrase assigns), so nothing here answers anybody's assignment. Each student made choices the assignment leaves open, such as whether to remove the offset and where to read the change in velocity. The technical report reads the change at the end of the impact pulse and finds a gap of 2.48 m/s. The other two read it at the end of the record and find 1.83 and 1.85 m/s (they differ in whether the offset was removed). All three agree on the facts Alice needs, and each says why it chose what it did. Please see the Example reports page for what every report needs and who it is written for.

Each report is followed by a short note on what it does well. The purpose of the notes is to show that three different styles can meet the same standard (plots identified at a glance, errors flagged, assumptions noted).

The technical report's program is read one section at a time, beside a MATLAB version that prints the same results, on Reading the assignment 3 program.

A Memo

Example 1 · Memo

To: Alice

Re: Impact speed and crush in test 9793

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Yes, the accelerometer and the lab's speed are consistent, and the gap between them is the car bouncing back off the barrier. Added up over the whole record, the accelerometer on the car body says its velocity changed by 17.44 m/s. The lab measured the car at 15.60 m/s (56.15 km/h) as it hit. The gap is about 1.8 m/s, or 12% of the impact speed. The passenger compartment moved 645 mm toward the barrier after contact before it stopped, so the crush is 645 mm.

An accelerometer records how fast the velocity is changing. Adding up its record over time (integrating it) gives the total change in velocity, and that total equals the impact speed only if the car ends the record standing still. It does not end still. Starting from the lab's speed, the car stops at 64.9 ms after contact and is moving backward at 1.85 m/s when the record ends at 300 ms. The average deceleration from contact to the stop is 24.5 g (one g is 9.81 m/s²).

Three things need your attention.

  • The rebound is inferred. Nothing in the file measures the backward speed on its own. It is what is left when the lab's speed is taken away from the accelerometer's change, so it is only as good as both of them.
  • The car slows on the way back. Its backward speed is largest, 2.48 m/s, at 85 ms and falls to 1.85 m/s by the end of the record. Something is slowing it after it leaves the barrier, and I cannot tell what from this one channel.
  • One sensor stands for the compartment. The only accelerometer on the car is on the left rear seat crossmember (the file calls it vehicle_x). The crush is how far that point moved.

Assumptions. Time zero is the moment of contact, as the file declares. Before contact the car is towed at a steady speed, so the 0.041 m/s² the sensor averages then is an offset, and I subtracted it. The compartment moves as one rigid piece, and the barrier does not move at all. I did not apply the CFC 180 filter that the SAE J211 standard calls for before integrating (a smoothing filter defined in the standard, which is next week's material). Integrating already averages out fast swings, and I expect the filter to change these numbers very little, although I have not checked.

Program. a3_impact_speed.py (attached) prints every number above and draws the figure.

What to notice

The memo answers Alice in its first word and gives both numbers and the gap in the same paragraph. It explains the gap in terms she can check against the figure (the car is still moving backward when the record ends). The list says plainly that the rebound is inferred and not measured. The assumptions name the filter the student chose not to apply, with the reason and an honest hedge.

A Technical Report

Example 2 · Technical report

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Summary

The crossmember accelerometer agrees with the lab's impact speed once the rebound is allowed for. Through the impact the accelerometer records a change in velocity of 18.08 m/s, against the lab's 15.60 m/s (56.15 km/h). The 2.48 m/s difference (15.9% of the impact speed) is the speed at which the crossmember was moving back from the barrier at 85.0 ms, when the barrier stopped pushing on it. None of the processing choices tested moves the change by more than 0.01 m/s, so the gap is not a product of the analysis. The passenger compartment travelled 645 mm toward the barrier before it stopped at 64.9 ms, and that distance is the crush.

Data

Test 9793 is a New Car Assessment test of a 2017 Hyundai Elantra, run head on into a rigid barrier. The lab measured the speed at contact as 56.15 km/h, which is 15.60 m/s. The report uses one channel, 10SEATLERE00ACXP, whose header describes an accelerometer on the left rear seat crossmember measuring along the car (X, forward positive). It is the only sensor on the car body. The other seven channels are on the dummies, which move relative to the car during the crash. The channel holds 7001 samples taken every 0.05 ms (a sample rate of 20,000 per second), from 50 ms before contact to 300 ms after. Before contact the channel averages 0.041 m/s² with a standard deviation of 2.513 m/s², which is what a sensor on a car towed at constant speed should read.

Method

A Python program, a3_velocity_and_crush.py (listed in the appendix and submitted with this report), does every step and draws every figure.

  1. Filter. The channel is not filtered. The standard SAE J211-1 assigns this channel the CFC 180 filter before integrating, and that filter is next week's material.
  2. Offset. The mean of the channel before contact is subtracted from every sample, since the true acceleration is zero while the car is towed.
  3. Change in velocity. The channel is integrated by the cumulative trapezoid rule, which adds up the area of a trapezoid between each pair of samples, and the result is set to zero at contact. This gives the change in velocity since contact (Figure 2). Integration cannot give the velocity itself without one known value, the constant of integration.
  4. Where to read it. The change is read where it is largest, at 85.0 ms. That is where the acceleration turns from rearward to forward at the end of the pulse. The barrier can push the car and cannot pull it, so the barrier's part of the event is probably over by then. The change over the whole record is also given.
  5. Velocity and crush. The lab's 15.60 m/s is added as the constant, which turns the change into the compartment's velocity. That velocity is integrated again from zero at contact to give the distance travelled. The crush is the distance at the first sample where the velocity reaches zero (Figure 3).

No choice moves either change by more than 0.01 m/s or the crush by more than 1 mm. The gap to the lab is about a hundred times larger, so it is not coming from the processing.

The constant. With the constant 0.1 m/s below or above the lab's speed, the crush is 639 mm or 652 mm. That is roughly the 0.1 m/s multiplied by the 65 ms to the stop, as The constant of integration predicts. Had the accelerometer's own 17.44 m/s been used as the constant, which assumes the car finished the record at rest, the velocity would reach zero at 73.3 ms and the crush would be 771 mm, 126 mm more. The crush depends far more on the constant than on any processing choice, and the lab's speed is the better constant because it was measured.

Anomalies

  • The rebound slows. From 85.0 to 150 ms the acceleration averages +0.77 g (one g is 9.81 m/s²), so the backward speed falls from 2.48 m/s to 1.85 m/s by the end of the record. The channel cannot say what does this.
  • The end of the record has not settled. The acceleration over the last 50 ms averages 0.636 m/s². This may be real motion or an offset the pre-contact mean did not remove, and the record is too short to tell. It is one reason the comparison uses the change at 85.0 ms.
  • Spikes in the channel. The recorded channel has sharp spikes (Figure 1). They are much faster than the pulse and are integrated as recorded. Next week's filter is the standard treatment for them.
  • The rebound is inferred. It is the difference between two numbers. No independent measurement of the rebound speed is in the file.

Assumptions

  • Time zero is the moment of contact. This is the zero the file declares.
  • The acceleration before contact is zero, so its recorded mean is an offset.
  • The crossmember moves with the whole passenger compartment, so its speed is the car's, and the barrier does not move.
  • The lab's speed is correct. The file gives no uncertainty for it.
  • The barrier stops pushing at the instant the change in velocity is largest.

Appendix. Program

"""Assignment 3: impact speed and crush distance from the crossmember accelerometer.

Run this from the unzipped pack folder. It prints every number in the report
(the comparison with the lab's speed, the crush, and the table of processing
choices) and saves the three figures:
    fig1_acceleration.svg   the channel with its pre-contact offset removed
    fig2_delta_v.svg        change in velocity since contact, constant set to zero
    fig3_velocity_crush.svg velocity with the lab's speed added, and distance
"""
import json
import os

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

G = 9.81   # m/s^2 in one g


def load_test(pack='.'):
    folder = os.path.join(pack, 'crash')
    name = os.listdir(folder)[0]          # the crash folder holds one file
    with open(os.path.join(folder, name)) as f:
        return json.load(f)


def running_integral(y, dt, rule='trapezoid'):
    """Cumulative integral with the leading zero, so it lines up with t."""
    if rule == 'trapezoid':
        panels = (y[1:] + y[:-1]) / 2 * dt
    else:                                  # rectangle, left-hand sample
        panels = y[:-1] * dt
    return np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum(panels)])


def analyse(a, t, dt, lab, baseline=True, rule='trapezoid'):
    """Everything the report needs from one set of processing choices."""
    if baseline:
        a = a - np.mean(a[t < 0])      # towed at constant speed before contact
    i0 = np.argmax(t >= 0)             # the first sample at or after contact
    dv = running_integral(a, dt, rule)
    dv = dv - dv[i0]                   # change since contact
    v = lab + dv                       # the lab's speed is the constant
    stop = i0 + np.argmax(v[i0:] <= 0)  # the first sample after contact with v at or below 0
    x = running_integral(v, dt, rule)
    x = x - x[i0]
    return {'a': a, 'dv': dv, 'v': v, 'x': x, 'stop': stop,
            'peak': np.argmin(dv), 'crush': x[stop]}


test = load_test()
ch = test['channels']['vehicle_x']      # V1 LEFT REAR SEAT CROSSMEMBER AX
dt = ch['dt']
t = ch['t0'] + dt * np.arange(ch['n'])
raw = np.array(ch['y'])
lab = test['test']['speed_kph'] / 3.6

print(f"channel {ch['code']}: {ch['comment']}, {ch['n']} samples every {dt} s")
print(f"lab speed {test['test']['speed_kph']} km/h = {lab:.2f} m/s")

before = raw[t < 0]
print(f"before contact: mean {np.mean(before):.3f} m/s^2, "
      f"standard deviation {np.std(before):.3f} m/s^2")

r = analyse(raw, t, dt, lab)
peak = r['peak']
stop = r['stop']
print(f"largest change in velocity {-r['dv'][peak]:.2f} m/s at {1000 * t[peak]:.1f} ms"
      f"  (gap to lab {-r['dv'][peak] - lab:.2f} m/s, "
      f"{100 * (-r['dv'][peak] - lab) / lab:.1f}%)")
print(f"change over the whole record {-r['dv'][-1]:.2f} m/s"
      f"  (gap to lab {-r['dv'][-1] - lab:.2f} m/s, "
      f"{100 * (-r['dv'][-1] - lab) / lab:.1f}%)")
after = (t > t[peak]) & (t <= 0.15)
print(f"mean acceleration from {1000 * t[peak]:.1f} to 150 ms: "
      f"{np.mean(r['a'][after]) / G:.2f} g")
print(f"mean acceleration over the last 50 ms: {np.mean(r['a'][t > 0.25]):.3f} m/s^2")
print(f"stopped at {1000 * t[stop]:.1f} ms, crush {1000 * r['crush']:.0f} mm")
print(f"velocity at end of record {r['v'][-1]:.2f} m/s")

# how much each processing choice moves the answers
print()
print(f"{'baseline':9} {'rule':10} {'dv peak':>8} {'dv end':>7} "
      f"{'stop ms':>8} {'crush mm':>9}")
choices = [(True, 'trapezoid'), (False, 'trapezoid'), (True, 'rectangle')]
for baseline, rule in choices:
    s = analyse(raw, t, dt, lab, baseline, rule)
    if baseline:
        label = 'yes'
    else:
        label = 'no'
    print(f"{label:9} "
          f"{rule:10} {-s['dv'][s['peak']]:8.2f} {-s['dv'][-1]:7.2f} "
          f"{1000 * t[s['stop']]:8.1f} {1000 * s['crush']:9.0f}")

# how much the constant moves the crush
print()
for c in [lab - 0.1, lab + 0.1, -r['dv'][-1]]:
    s = analyse(raw, t, dt, c)
    print(f"constant {c:.2f} m/s: stop {1000 * t[s['stop']]:.1f} ms, "
          f"crush {1000 * s['crush']:.0f} mm")

t_ms = 1000 * t

# Figure 1: the channel in g, offset removed
plt.figure(figsize=(7, 3.6))
plt.plot(t_ms, r['a'] / G, linewidth=0.7, label='as recorded, offset removed')
plt.axvline(0, color='gray', linestyle='--', linewidth=0.8)
plt.xlim(-20, 160)
plt.xlabel('time from contact (ms)')
plt.ylabel('acceleration (g)')
plt.title('Left rear seat crossmember, X acceleration [10SEATLERE00ACXP]')
plt.legend(loc='lower right')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.savefig('fig1_acceleration.svg')
plt.close()

# Figure 2: change in velocity, constant set to zero
plt.figure(figsize=(7, 3.6))
plt.plot(t_ms, r['dv'])
plt.axhline(-lab, color='gray', linestyle='--', linewidth=0.8)
plt.text(295, -lab + 0.6, f"minus the lab's speed, {-lab:.2f} m/s", ha='right')
plt.plot(t_ms[peak], r['dv'][peak], 'o')
plt.text(t_ms[peak] + 4, r['dv'][peak] - 1.6,
         f"{r['dv'][peak]:.2f} m/s at {t_ms[peak]:.1f} ms")
plt.plot(t_ms[-1], r['dv'][-1], 'o')
plt.text(295, r['dv'][-1] - 0.8, f"{r['dv'][-1]:.2f} m/s at 300 ms",
         ha='right', va='top')
plt.ylim(-21, 2)
plt.xlabel('time from contact (ms)')
plt.ylabel('change in velocity (m/s)')
plt.title('Change in velocity since contact (crossmember, constant = 0)')
plt.tight_layout()
plt.savefig('fig2_delta_v.svg')
plt.close()

# Figure 3: velocity with the lab's constant, and distance
fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 1, figsize=(7, 6), sharex=True)
top = axes[0]
bottom = axes[1]
top.plot(t_ms, r['v'])
top.axhline(0, color='gray', linewidth=0.8)
top.plot(t_ms[stop], 0, 'o')
top.text(t_ms[stop] + 4, 1.0, f"zero at {t_ms[stop]:.1f} ms")
top.set_ylabel('velocity (m/s)')
top.set_title('(a) Velocity of the compartment, lab speed as the constant')
bottom.plot(t_ms, 1000 * r['x'])
bottom.plot(t_ms[stop], 1000 * r['crush'], 'o')
bottom.text(t_ms[stop] + 4, 1000 * r['crush'] - 90, f"{1000 * r['crush']:.0f} mm")
bottom.set_ylim(-100, 750)
bottom.set_xlim(-10, 160)
bottom.set_xlabel('time from contact (ms)')
bottom.set_ylabel('distance since contact (mm)')
bottom.set_title('(b) Distance travelled toward the barrier')
fig.tight_layout()
fig.savefig('fig3_velocity_crush.svg')
What to notice

The summary gives the answer, both numbers and the gap before any method. The table of processing choices is the evidence that the gap is physical (no choice moves the change by more than 0.01 m/s). The check on the constant shows the reader which decision the crush actually depends on, with 771 mm beside 645 mm.

Assignment 3, impact speed and crush. Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Answer for Alice, worked out below. The accelerometer's total change in velocity is 17.43 m/s and the lab measured 15.60 m/s, a gap of 1.83 m/s. I think the gap is the car bouncing back off the barrier, so yes, they agree once that is allowed for. The crush is 645 mm.

I did this in Google Colab. The hint says the channel on the car body is the one whose header names a crossmember, which is vehicle_x. First, load it and get the lab's speed in m/s.

import json
import numpy as np

with open('crash/09793.json') as f:
    test = json.load(f)

ch = test['channels']['vehicle_x']
print(ch['comment'], ch['code'], ch['unit'])
print(test['test'])
lab = test['test']['speed_kph'] / 3.6
print('lab speed:', round(lab, 3), 'm/s')

Next, a time for every sample and a look at the channel. I divided by 9.81 to plot it in g, since crash accelerations are usually quoted that way.

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

dt = ch['dt']
t = ch['t0'] + dt * np.arange(ch['n'])

plt.plot(1000 * t, a / 9.81)

It is very spiky. The SAE J211 standard says to filter this channel at CFC 180 before integrating (a smoothing filter), but that is next week. Integrating adds up area, so the fast spikes should mostly cancel. I am leaving it unfiltered, although I have not checked how much the filter would change the answer.

Now integrate. I used the cumulative trapezoid function from the notes, with the leading zero so it lines up with t, and subtracted its value at contact so it starts from zero there. This is the change in velocity since contact, with no constant added yet.

def cumulative(y, dt):
    panels = (y[1:] + y[:-1]) * 0.5 * dt
    running = np.cumsum(panels)
    return np.concatenate([[0.0], running])

i0 = np.argmax(t >= 0)       # the first sample at or after contact
dv = cumulative(a, dt)
dv = dv - dv[i0]
print('change in velocity, contact to end:', round(dv[-1], 3), 'm/s')
print('largest change:', round(np.min(dv), 3), 'm/s at',
      round(1000 * t[np.argmin(dv)], 1), 'ms')
print('gap to the lab:', round(-dv[-1] - lab, 3), 'm/s')
change in velocity, contact to end: -17.431 m/s
largest change: -18.077 m/s at 85.0 ms
gap to the lab: 1.833 m/s

The car lost 17.431 m/s over the record, 1.833 m/s more than the lab's speed. If the car were standing still at the end, the change would equal the impact speed exactly. The largest change comes at 85 ms and then it shrinks a little, which looks like the car moving backward after the hit. I am comparing the whole-record change with the lab, because then the gap is just the speed the car still has when the record stops, which I can read straight off a velocity plot.

Before trusting that, I checked whether an offset in the sensor could explain the gap. Before contact the car is towed at a steady speed, so the sensor should read zero. Anything it reads then is an offset, and integrating an offset adds the offset times the time.

b = np.mean(a[t < 0])
T = t[-1] - t[i0]
print('offset before contact:', round(b, 4), 'm/s^2')
print('noise before contact (std):', round(np.std(a[t < 0]), 3), 'm/s^2')
print('offset times', round(T, 2), 's:', round(b * T, 4), 'm/s')
offset before contact: 0.0411 m/s^2
noise before contact (std): 2.513 m/s^2
offset times 0.3 s: 0.0123 m/s

The offset only adds 0.0123 m/s over the record, under 1% of the gap, so it cannot explain it. I left it in. Now add the lab's speed as the constant of integration (the one known velocity that turns a change into an actual velocity).

v = lab + dv
stop = i0 + np.argmax(v[i0:] <= 0)   # the first sample after contact with v at or below 0
print('velocity reaches zero at', round(1000 * t[stop], 2), 'ms')
print('velocity at end of record:', round(v[-1], 3), 'm/s')

plt.plot(1000 * t, v)
plt.axhline(0, color='gray')
plt.xlabel('time from contact (ms)')
plt.ylabel('velocity (m/s)')
plt.title('Crossmember velocity, lab speed added as the constant')
plt.show()
velocity reaches zero at 64.85 ms
velocity at end of record: -1.833 m/s

The velocity goes through zero at 64.85 ms and ends at −1.833 m/s, so the car is moving backward at the end. The backward speed is the same 1.833 m/s as the gap, which has to be true since I built the curve from the lab's speed. That backward speed is the rebound. Integrating again gives the distance the crossmember moved after contact.

x = cumulative(v, dt)
x = x - x[i0]
print('distance at the stop:', round(1000 * x[stop], 1), 'mm')
print('largest distance:', round(1000 * np.max(x), 1), 'mm')
print('distance at end of record:', round(1000 * x[-1], 1), 'mm')

plt.plot(1000 * t, 1000 * x)
plt.ylim(-100, 700)
plt.xlabel('time from contact (ms)')
plt.ylabel('distance since contact (mm)')
plt.title('Crossmember distance toward the barrier')
plt.show()
distance at the stop: 645.2 mm
largest distance: 645.2 mm
distance at end of record: 173.0 mm

The distance peaks at 645.2 mm, at the same instant the velocity reaches zero, and then falls to 173.0 mm by the end. The fall is the car rolling back away from the wall, so the crush is the peak and not the final value. Last, I checked that the numbers are physically sensible.

print('average deceleration to the stop:', round(lab / t[stop] / 9.81, 1), 'g')
print('steady deceleration over the same distance:',
      round(lab**2 / (2 * x[stop]) / 9.81, 1), 'g')
print('rebound speed / impact speed at end of record:', round(-v[-1] / lab, 3))
average deceleration to the stop: 24.5 g
steady deceleration over the same distance: 19.2 g
rebound speed / impact speed at end of record: 0.118

The steady deceleration that would stop the car in 645 mm is 19.2 g, close to the 24.5 g average to the stop, so the two calculations agree on the size of the pulse. The real pulse is not steady, so I did not expect them to match exactly. The Bias and noise notes found a rebound of 0.119 of the impact speed for a different car (test 10642), so 0.118 is the same size, although that is only one other test.

Flags. The rebound is inferred from the gap and not measured by anything in the file. The car's backward speed is largest at 85 ms and drops after that, and I do not know why. Assumptions. Time zero is contact. The crossmember moves with the whole passenger compartment, and the barrier does not move. The lab's speed is right. I did not filter the channel or remove the 0.0411 m/s² offset, since the offset is worth 0.0123 m/s.

What to notice

The notebook puts its answer in the first paragraph and then shows the work in the order it was done. It tests the most obvious other explanation for the gap (a sensor offset) with its own numbers before accepting the rebound. The plausibility check at the end is a good habit, and it is probably the quickest way to catch a units error in a result like this.
