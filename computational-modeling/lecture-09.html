Computational Physical Modeling

A derivative from sampled data

A camera system recorded this shot's height 25 times a second (about every 0.04 s).

The ball's vertical speed is the slope of this curve.

From two samples, a slope is the rise divided by the run.

Computational Physical Modeling

Forward, backward and central differences

Each rule divides a difference of two samples by the time between them.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{forward:}&\quad f'(x) \approx \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\\[4pt] \text{backward:}&\quad f'(x) \approx \frac{f(x) - f(x-h)}{h}\\[4pt] \text{central:}&\quad f'(x) \approx \frac{f(x+h) - f(x-h)}{2h} \end{aligned}$$

At frame 25 the three rules give three different speeds.

On a smooth curve the central chord is usually closest to the tangent.

The central difference is the average of the other two.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} + \frac{f(x) - f(x-h)}{h}\right] &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{\frac{f(x+h) - f(x-h)}{2h}}\\[18pt] \text{frame 25:}\quad \tfrac12\,(2.245 + 3.140) &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{2.6925\ \text{m/s}} \end{aligned}$$

Computational Physical Modeling

The error in each rule, from a Taylor expansion

A Taylor expansion gives $f$ a step away from what is known at $x$.

$$f(x+h) \;=\; \underbrace{f(x)}_{\text{value}} \;+\; \underbrace{h\,f'(x)}_{\text{slope}} \;+\; \underbrace{\tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(x)}_{\text{curvature}} \;+\; \tfrac{h^3}{6}f'''(x) \;+\; \dots$$

Solved for $f'(x)$, the expansion gives the forward difference and its error.

f(x+h) = f(x) + h f′(x) + h²2 f″(x) + … the expansion f(x+h) − f(x) = f(x) + h f′(x) + h²2 f″(x) + … − f(x) subtract f(x) from both sides f(x+h) − f(x)h = h f′(x)h + h h2 h f″(x) + … divide every term by h f(x+h) − f(x)h the formula we compute − h2 f″(x) − … what it leaves out: the error = f′(x) + h2 f″(x) + … − h2 f″(x) − … subtract the error from both sides, then swap the sides

On the ball, the curvature term is what a straight line misses.

A step forward and a step back differ only in the sign of the odd powers of $h$.

$$\begin{aligned} f(x+h) &= f(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;+\; h f'(x)} + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;+\; \tfrac{h^3}{6}f'''(x)} + \dots\\[4pt] f(x-h) &= f(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;-\; h f'(x)} + \tfrac{h^2}{2}f''(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;-\; \tfrac{h^3}{6}f'''(x)} + \dots \end{aligned}$$

The leading forward and backward errors are equal and opposite.

$$\begin{aligned} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h} &= f'(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;+\; \tfrac{h}{2}f''(x)} + \tfrac{h^2}{6}f'''(x) + \dots\\[4pt] \frac{f(x) - f(x-h)}{h} &= f'(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;-\; \tfrac{h}{2}f''(x)} + \tfrac{h^2}{6}f'''(x) - \dots \end{aligned}$$

Averaging cancels the $h$ term and leaves an error in $h^2$.

$$\frac{f(x+h) - f(x-h)}{2h} \;=\; f'(x) \textcolor{#8A6508}{\;+\; \tfrac{h^2}{6}f'''(x)} + \dots$$

One-sided error is linear in $h$ and central error is quadratic in $h$.

$$\text{one-sided: } \tfrac{h}{2}f''(x) \qquad\qquad \text{central: } \tfrac{h^2}{6}f'''(x)$$

Computational Physical Modeling

Vertical speed over a whole shot

The speeds roughly follow a line falling 9.81 m/s each second.

About 3 cm of tracking noise gives about 0.5 m/s of scatter.

Differencing again gives the acceleration, and the noise grows.

One frame at a time these scatter by about 27 m/s². The average of all 25 is −8.7 m/s².

$$a_i \;\approx\; \frac{z_{i+1} - 2z_i + z_{i-1}}{h^2}$$

The 9.81 line came from a model, a fit and a noise estimate, and not only from differencing.
