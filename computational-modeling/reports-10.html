A Memo

Example 1 · Memo

To: Alice

Re: Where the customer's car sits among the cars we hold

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

The customer's car sits in the middle of the three cars in the building, and all three are well inside the limit. Its driver HIC15 is 150.4, which is 21.5% of the FMVSS 208 limit of 700. The 2016 Honda Civic came out at 148.5 and the 2016 Buick Cascada at 187.5, so the three cars used between 21% and 27% of the limit.

HIC15 (the Head Injury Criterion) is the number the federal standard uses for head injury in a crash. It comes from the driver dummy's head acceleration, measured in g (multiples of gravity, 9.81 m/s²), averaged over the worst stretch of up to 15 ms. Higher is worse, and above 700 a car fails. I computed it the same way for all three cars: each head axis filtered at CFC 1000 (the smoothing filter the standard sets for head channels), combined into one total acceleration, and every window up to 15 ms searched for the largest value.

The picture supports two statements about these tests. All three cars are far below the limit, and the Buick's driver took a noticeably harder head load than the other two. It does not support calling the customer's car better or worse than the Civic. They differ by 1.9, and with one test of each car I cannot tell how far a repeat test would move either number, or whether even the gap to the Buick would hold. Three cars are also not a fleet in the market sense, so I would not tell the customer where the car sits among cars in general.

Four things need your attention.

  • The tests are not labeled the same type. The customer's file says NEW CAR ASSESSMENT TEST and the other two say OPTIONAL NEW CAR ASSESSMENT TEST. All three are head on into a barrier at 56.15 to 56.21 km/h, so I have treated them as the same test. I have not found out what "optional" means here.
  • The sample rates differ. The customer's car was recorded at 20,000 samples a second and the other two at 10,000. I computed each car at its own rate. Both rates are far above what the head filter keeps, so I doubt it matters, but I have not checked.
  • The channel descriptions are worded differently. Ours says V1P1 HEAD CG X ACCELERATION and the colleagues' files say DRIVER HEAD X. The channel codes are the same in all three (11HEADCG0000ACXP and its Y and Z), so I matched the channels by code.
  • The Buick's model name has a trailing space ('CASCADA '). It is harmless, and I removed it in the labels.

Assumptions. The three driver dummies are the same kind of dummy, since the files do not say. Time zero in each file is the moment of impact. The colleagues' files are the lab's data as recorded, and I have not inspected their other channels.

Program. a9_fleet_chart.py (attached) reads our test and the two in the fleet folder, prints a line for each car and draws the figure.

What to notice

The answer is the first sentence, and the number comes with the limit it should be read against. The memo draws the full scale up to 700, so Alice sees at once how far all three cars are from failing. It also says plainly what three cars cannot support (a ranking against the Civic, and any claim about cars in general).

A Technical Report

Example 2 · Technical report

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Summary

The customer's car, a 2017 Hyundai Elantra (NHTSA test 9793), has a driver HIC15 of 150.4. It is second lowest of the three cars in the building, 1.9 above a 2016 Honda Civic (148.5) and 37.1 below a 2016 Buick Cascada (187.5). The highest of the three is 26.8% of the FMVSS 208 limit of 700. The order holds under every check in this report, but the gap to the Civic is small enough that one test of each car probably cannot separate them.

Data

Three frontal barrier tests, one per car. The customer's test is in the pack and the other two came from colleagues. HIC15 (the Head Injury Criterion over windows up to 15 ms) is computed from the driver dummy's head acceleration in g, where one g is 9.80665 m/s². The table gives each test's speed and sample rate from its header, beside its results. The sample rate is how many readings the instrument takes per second. All three are head on (angle 0) into a barrier, and every channel reports a status of ok.

98082016 Honda Civic56.17 km/h10,000/s148.571.10 to 86.10 ms

Method

A Python program, a9_benchmark.py (listed in the appendix and submitted with this report), treats all three files identically. It picks the three driver head axes by channel code, filters each at CFC 1000 (the channel filter class SAE J211-1 sets for head acceleration, run forward then backward so the peaks do not shift), forms the resultant and divides by 9.80665. It then searches every window up to 15 ms for the largest value of HIC, using a running integral so each window costs one subtraction. These steps are the ones FMVSS 208 and J211-1 fix (see the standards).

Results

Figure 1 places the three cars on a scale close enough to see the gaps. The customer's car is second of three, and with three cars the median is the customer's car itself, so the median says nothing extra here. Figure 2 shows where each number comes from. The three peaks are within 2 g of each other (43.8 to 45.8 g), and the HIC15 values still differ by up to 39. HIC rewards a high acceleration held over the whole window, so the Buick's head was probably loaded hard for longer, although I have not measured the duration separately. The Civic's window opens about 10 ms later than the other two, following its later peak (76.7 ms, against 66.5 and 70.1 ms).

Checks

The ranking could be an artifact of how the numbers were computed, so three things were changed and the HIC recomputed. HIC36 is the older version of the criterion, with windows up to 36 ms.

The different sample rate does not explain anything, since recording the customer's car at the others' rate (keeping every second sample) leaves 150.4 unchanged. The filter moves no value by more than 0.2. Under HIC36 the order is the same and the gap to the Civic grows from 1.9 to 23.1. None of these is a repeat test, though, so they show the order belongs to the data and not to the method. They do not show it would survive a second crash of each car.

Errors and Anomalies

  • Test type. The customer's test is labeled NEW CAR ASSESSMENT TEST and the other two OPTIONAL NEW CAR ASSESSMENT TEST. The configuration, angle and speed agree, so they are compared here as the same test.
  • Channel descriptions. The customer's file describes the head channel as V1P1 HEAD CG X ACCELERATION and the other two as DRIVER HEAD X. The codes match, and channels were selected by code.
  • Model name. The Buick's model field is 'CASCADA ', with a trailing space. It was stripped for labels.

Assumptions

  • The three driver dummies are of the same type. The files do not say.
  • Position 1 (the 11 at the start of each code) is the driver in all three tests.
  • Time zero is the moment of impact in each file.
  • The three cars are worth comparing. They are 2016 and 2017 cars run under the same configuration, but nothing in the files says whether the customer would think of them as competitors.

What the Comparison Supports

It supports saying that in these tests the customer's car is in line with these two cars, well under the limit, and 37.1 below the Buick. It does not support ranking the customer's car above or below the Civic, or placing it in the market, and one test per car cannot show whether even the Buick gap would survive a repeat test. Three tests were not chosen to represent anything, and the median of three is just the middle car.

Appendix. Program

"""Assignment 9: benchmarking the customer's HIC15 against the other cars.

Run this from the unzipped pack folder. The customer's test is in crash/ and the
tests from the two colleagues are in fleet/. For each car the program:
  - reads the header (vehicle, test type, speed, sample rate),
  - filters the three driver head axes at CFC 1000, resultant in g,
  - finds HIC15 and its window, and HIC36 for comparison,
then runs two checks (sample rate and filter) and saves fig1_fleet.svg
(the HIC15 values) and fig2_resultants.svg (the head traces).
"""
import glob
import json
import math
import os

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

G = 9.80665      # standard gravity, m/s^2
LIMIT = 700      # FMVSS 208 limit on HIC15
PACK = '.'


def cfc(y, dt, cls):
    """SAE J211-1 filter class: 4-pole Butterworth, forward then backward."""
    wd = 2 * math.pi * cls * 2.0775
    wa = math.tan(wd * dt / 2)
    d = 1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa + wa ** 2
    a0 = wa ** 2 / d
    b1 = -2 * (wa ** 2 - 1) / d
    b2 = (-1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa - wa ** 2) / d

    def one_pass(x):
        out = np.zeros_like(x)
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            out[i] = (a0 * x[i] + 2 * a0 * x[i - 1] + a0 * x[i - 2]
                      + b1 * out[i - 1] + b2 * out[i - 2])
        return out
    return one_pass(one_pass(y)[::-1])[::-1]


def hic(a_g, dt, limit_ms):
    """HIC over every window up to limit_ms. Returns (value, (first, last))."""
    S = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum((a_g[1:] + a_g[:-1]) / 2 * dt)])
    best, window = 0.0, (0, 0)
    for k in range(1, int(round(limit_ms / 1000 / dt)) + 1):
        T = k * dt
        v = T * np.clip((S[k:] - S[:-k]) / T, 0, None) ** 2.5
        i = int(np.argmax(v))
        if v[i] > best:
            best, window = float(v[i]), (i, i + k)
    return best, window


def resultant(axes, dt, cls=1000):
    """Filter each axis (cls=None skips it), then the resultant in g."""
    if cls:
        axes = [cfc(a, dt, cls) for a in axes]
    return np.sqrt(axes[0] ** 2 + axes[1] ** 2 + axes[2] ** 2) / G


cars = []
paths = glob.glob(os.path.join(PACK, 'crash', '*.json'))
paths += sorted(glob.glob(os.path.join(PACK, 'fleet', '*.json')))
for path in paths:
    with open(path) as f:
        test = json.load(f)
    ch = {c['code']: c for c in test['channels'].values()}
    hx = ch['11HEADCG0000ACXP']
    dt, t0 = hx['dt'], hx['t0']
    axes = [np.array(ch['11HEADCG0000AC' + a + 'P']['y']) for a in 'XYZ']
    res = resultant(axes, dt)
    t_ms = 1000 * (t0 + dt * np.arange(len(res)))
    h15, (i, j) = hic(res, dt, 15)
    v, info = test['vehicle'], test['test']
    cars.append(dict(
        no=test['testNo'], mine='crash' in path, info=info, model=v['model'],
        name=f"{v['year']} {v['make'].title()} {v['model'].strip().title()}",
        axes=axes, dt=dt, t=t_ms, res=res, h15=h15, h36=hic(res, dt, 36)[0],
        win=(t_ms[i], t_ms[j]), peak=res.max(), peak_t=t_ms[np.argmax(res)],
        descr=hx['comment'], status={c['status'] for c in ch.values()}))

for c in cars:
    info = c['info']
    print(f"{c['no']} {c['name']}  (model field {c['model']!r})")
    print(f"   {info['type']}, {info['config']}, angle {info['angle']}, "
          f"{info['speed_kph']} km/h")
    print(f"   {1 / c['dt']:.0f} samples/s, head X described as {c['descr']!r}, "
          f"status {c['status']}")
    print(f"   HIC15 {c['h15']:.1f}, window {c['win'][0]:.2f} to {c['win'][1]:.2f} ms,"
          f" HIC36 {c['h36']:.1f}, peak {c['peak']:.1f} g at {c['peak_t']:.1f} ms")

h15 = sorted(c['h15'] for c in cars)
me = [c for c in cars if c['mine']][0]
print(f"\ncustomer's car is {h15.index(me['h15']) + 1} of {len(cars)}, lowest first")
print(f"range {h15[0]:.1f} to {h15[-1]:.1f}, median {np.median(h15):.1f}, "
      f"highest is {100 * h15[-1] / LIMIT:.1f}% of the limit")
for c in cars:
    if not c['mine']:
        print(f"customer minus {c['name']}: HIC15 {me['h15'] - c['h15']:+.1f}, "
              f"HIC36 {me['h36'] - c['h36']:+.1f}")

# check 1: the customer's car was sampled twice as fast as the other two.
# Keep every second sample (10,000 a second, like the others) and recompute.
half = resultant([a[::2] for a in me['axes']], 2 * me['dt'])
print(f"\ncheck, customer's car at half the sample rate: "
      f"HIC15 {hic(half, 2 * me['dt'], 15)[0]:.1f}")
# check 2: no filter at all, every car
for c in cars:
    raw = resultant(c['axes'], c['dt'], None)
    print(f"check, {c['name']} with no filter: HIC15 {hic(raw, c['dt'], 15)[0]:.1f}")

# Figure 1: the three HIC15 values on a scale close enough to see the gaps
order = sorted(cars, key=lambda c: c['h15'])
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 2.6))
for k, c in enumerate(order):
    ax.plot(c['h15'], k, 'o', ms=9, color='tab:orange' if c['mine'] else 'tab:blue')
    ax.annotate(f"{c['h15']:.1f}", (c['h15'], k), xytext=(0, 9),
                textcoords='offset points', ha='center')
ax.set_yticks(range(len(order)))
ax.set_yticklabels([c['name'] + (' (customer)' if c['mine'] else '') for c in order])
ax.set_ylim(-0.6, len(order) - 0.2)
ax.set_xlim(100, 220)
ax.set_xlabel('driver HIC15, CFC 1000 (no unit; the limit, 700, is off this scale)')
ax.set_title('Figure 1. Driver HIC15, three cars, same barrier, about 56 km/h',
             fontsize=10)
ax.grid(axis='x', alpha=0.3)
fig.savefig('fig1_fleet.svg', bbox_inches='tight')

# Figure 2: the head resultant for each car with its HIC15 window shaded
fig, axs = plt.subplots(3, 1, figsize=(7, 7), sharex=True, sharey=True)
for ax, c in zip(axs, cars):
    ax.plot(c['t'], c['res'], linewidth=0.9)
    ax.axvspan(c['win'][0], c['win'][1], color='tab:orange', alpha=0.25)
    ax.set_title(f"{c['name']} (test {c['no']}): HIC15 {c['h15']:.1f}, "
                 f"window {c['win'][0]:.1f} to {c['win'][1]:.1f} ms", fontsize=9)
    ax.set_ylabel('head resultant (g)')
axs[-1].set_xlim(-10, 200)
axs[-1].set_xlabel('time from impact (ms)')
fig.suptitle('Figure 2. Driver head resultant, CFC 1000, HIC15 window shaded',
             fontsize=10)
fig.tight_layout()
fig.savefig('fig2_resultants.svg', bbox_inches='tight')

Now HIC15 for each car. HIC is the Head Injury Criterion, the head acceleration in g averaged over a window, raised to the power 2.5 and multiplied by the window length, with the worst window up to 15 ms taken. I filter each head axis at CFC 1000 first, which is the filter class the standard gives head channels.

import math

G = 9.80665

def cfc(y, dt, cls):
    # SAE J211-1 filter, 4-pole Butterworth, forward then backward
    wd = 2 * math.pi * cls * 2.0775
    wa = math.tan(wd * dt / 2)
    d = 1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa + wa ** 2
    a0 = wa ** 2 / d
    b1 = -2 * (wa ** 2 - 1) / d
    b2 = (-1 + math.sqrt(2) * wa - wa ** 2) / d
    def one_pass(x):
        out = np.zeros_like(x)
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            out[i] = (a0*x[i] + 2*a0*x[i-1] + a0*x[i-2]
                      + b1*out[i-1] + b2*out[i-2])
        return out
    return one_pass(one_pass(y)[::-1])[::-1]

def hic15(a, dt):
    # running integral, then every window from 1 sample up to 15 ms
    S = np.concatenate([[0.0], np.cumsum((a[1:] + a[:-1]) / 2 * dt)])
    best, win = 0.0, (0, 0)
    for k in range(1, round(0.015 / dt) + 1):
        v = k * dt * np.clip((S[k:] - S[:-k]) / (k * dt), 0, None) ** 2.5
        i = int(np.argmax(v))
        if v[i] > best:
            best, win = float(v[i]), (i, i + k)
    return best, win

results = []
for t in tests:
    chs = t['channels']
    dt, t0 = chs['head_x']['dt'], chs['head_x']['t0']
    heads = [np.array(chs[k]['y']) for k in ('head_x', 'head_y', 'head_z')]
    x, y, z = [cfc(a, dt, 1000) for a in heads]
    res = np.sqrt(x**2 + y**2 + z**2) / G
    h, (i, j) = hic15(res, dt)
    t_ms = 1000 * (t0 + dt * np.arange(len(res)))
    name = f"{t['vehicle']['make'].title()} {t['vehicle']['model'].strip().title()}"
    results.append((name, h, t_ms, res, t_ms[i], t_ms[j]))
    print(f"{name:16} HIC15 {h:.1f}  ({100 * h / 700:.1f}% of 700)")
    print(f"    window {t_ms[i]:.2f} to {t_ms[j]:.2f} ms, "
          f"peak {res.max():.1f} g at {t_ms[np.argmax(res)]:.1f} ms")
