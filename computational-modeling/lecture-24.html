Euler’s method, stepping a tracked ball forward in time

A fitted line and parabola say where the ball comes down through the rim, and so whether it goes in clean, hits the rim or misses.

Fitted to all 30 frames from release to the last one above the rim.

Fitted to only the first 6, 11 or 16 frames, the parabola comes down 0.77, 0.50 or 0.31 m from the hoop’s center, and the ball came through at 0.06 m.

A prediction can use only the frames tracked so far.

With drag added, the same fits come down closer to where the ball came through, and the 16-frame fit comes down 0.08 m from the center, clean.

We read r₀ and v₀ at the middle of the fitted frames, 0.20 s after release, where we expect the fit to be best.

With gravity alone the fit projects a straight line across the floor and a parabola in height, 25 cm from the ball 0.96 s after release.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{across the floor:}\quad (x,\ y) &= (x_0,\ y_0) + (v_{x0},\ v_{y0})\,(t - t_0) \\[4pt] \text{height:}\quad z &= z_0 + v_{z0}\,(t - t_0) \;-\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,(t - t_0)^2, \qquad t_0 = 0.20\ \text{s},\ g = 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2 \end{aligned}$$

Air drag pushes against the ball’s motion, with a force that grows with the speed squared.

Newton’s second law: the ball’s mass times its acceleration equals the sum of the forces on it.

$$m\,\mathbf{a} \;=\; \textcolor{#5F7038}{\mathbf{F}_{\text{gravity}}} \;+\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{\mathbf{F}_{\text{drag}}}$$

Gravity pulls with the ball’s weight, m g, straight down, so on its own it gives every ball the same acceleration, 9.81 m/s² down.

$$\textcolor{#5F7038}{\mathbf{F}_{\text{gravity}}} = -m g\,\hat{\mathbf{z}} \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad \frac{\textcolor{#5F7038}{\mathbf{F}_{\text{gravity}}}}{m} = -g\,\hat{\mathbf{z}} = (0,\ 0,\ -9.81)\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The drag force is ½ρCDA times the speed squared, about 0.53 N at the start, and a quarter of that at half the speed.

$$|\textcolor{#8A6508}{\mathbf{F}_{\text{drag}}}| \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\;\underbrace{\;\rho\;}_{\textstyle\begin{array}{c}\text{air density}\\ 1.20\ \text{kg/m}^3\end{array}}\;\;\underbrace{\;C_D\;}_{\textstyle\begin{array}{c}\text{drag coefficient}\\ 0.35\end{array}}\;\;\underbrace{\;A\;}_{\textstyle\begin{array}{c}\text{cross-section}\\ 0.0449\ \text{m}^2\end{array}}\;\;\underbrace{|\mathbf{v}|^2}_{\textstyle\begin{array}{c}\text{speed}\\ \text{squared}\end{array}}$$

Drag points against the velocity, so the drag force is its size times the direction opposite the motion.

$$\textcolor{#8A6508}{\mathbf{F}_{\text{drag}}} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\rho\, C_D A\,|\mathbf{v}|^2 \left(-\frac{\mathbf{v}}{|\mathbf{v}|}\right) \;=\; -\tfrac{1}{2}\rho\, C_D A\,|\mathbf{v}|\,\mathbf{v}$$

Dividing by the mass gives the acceleration, with the air and the ball collected in one constant k, about 0.016 per meter.

$$\begin{aligned} \mathbf{a} &= \textcolor{#5F7038}{-g\,\hat{\mathbf{z}}} \;\textcolor{#8A6508}{-\; k\,|\mathbf{v}|\,\mathbf{v}} \\[4pt] k &= \frac{\rho\, C_D A}{2m} = \frac{1.20 \times 0.35 \times 0.0449}{2 \times 0.600} \\[4pt] &= 0.0157 \approx 0.016\ \text{per m} \\[8pt] k\,|\mathbf{v}| &= 0.016 \times 7.52 = 0.1203\ \text{per s} \\[4pt] \mathbf{a} &= \textcolor{#5F7038}{(0,\ 0,\ -9.81)} \;\textcolor{#8A6508}{-\; 0.1203\,(-5.069,\ -3.910,\ 3.945)} \\[4pt] &= (0.610,\ 0.470,\ -10.285)\ \text{m/s}^2 \end{aligned}$$

With drag the acceleration depends on the velocity, so it changes all through the flight.

With drag the three directions are coupled through the speed, so the path has no formula in ordinary functions.

$$\begin{aligned} \text{gravity alone:}\quad \frac{d^2\mathbf{r}}{dt^2} &= -g\,\hat{\mathbf{z}} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_0 + \mathbf{v}_0\,(t - t_0) - \tfrac{1}{2}\,g\,(t - t_0)^2\,\hat{\mathbf{z}} \\[6pt] \text{with drag:}\quad \frac{d^2\mathbf{r}}{dt^2} &= -g\,\hat{\mathbf{z}} \;\textcolor{#8A6508}{-\; k\,\left|\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\right|\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}} \\[6pt] \text{in } x\text{, with } \mathbf{v} = \tfrac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\text{:}\quad \frac{dv_x}{dt} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{-k\,\sqrt{v_x^2 + v_y^2 + v_z^2}\;v_x} \end{aligned}$$

The velocity is the rate of change of the position, and the acceleration is the rate of change of the velocity.

$$\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} = \mathbf{v} \qquad\qquad \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt} = \mathbf{a}$$

We choose a small time step h, because over a short enough time each curve is nearly a straight line.

We step with that straight line, holding v and a fixed for one step, so the position changes by v h and the velocity by a h.

Each step updates all six numbers, each position by h times its velocity and each velocity by h times its acceleration.

i counts the steps from the start, so step i is at ti = 0.20 + i h. The accelerations come from the forces at the start of the step.

$$\begin{aligned} x_{i+1} &= x_i + h\,v_{x,i} &\qquad v_{x,i+1} &= v_{x,i} + h\,a_{x,i} &\qquad a_{x,i} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{-k\,|\mathbf{v}_i|\,v_{x,i}} \\[6pt] y_{i+1} &= y_i + h\,v_{y,i} & v_{y,i+1} &= v_{y,i} + h\,a_{y,i} & a_{y,i} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{-k\,|\mathbf{v}_i|\,v_{y,i}} \\[6pt] z_{i+1} &= z_i + h\,v_{z,i} & v_{z,i+1} &= v_{z,i} + h\,a_{z,i} & a_{z,i} &= -g\;\textcolor{#8A6508}{-\;k\,|\mathbf{v}_i|\,v_{z,i}} \end{aligned}$$

Stacked into one state, the six updates become one rule, Euler’s method: the new state is the old state plus h times its rate.

The state s is the six numbers, the ball’s position and velocity, and si is the state after i steps, at time ti. The rate function f(t, s) takes the time and the state and returns the six rates, the three velocities and the three accelerations from the forces.

$$s_{i+1} \;=\; s_i \;+\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{h\, f(t_i,\, s_i)} \qquad\qquad \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \\ v_x \\ v_y \\ v_z \end{bmatrix}_{i+1} = \begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \\ v_x \\ v_y \\ v_z \end{bmatrix}_{i} + \;\textcolor{#8A6508}{h \begin{bmatrix} v_x \\ v_y \\ v_z \\ a_x \\ a_y \\ a_z \end{bmatrix}_{i}}$$

With drag, 40 ms steps end 19 cm above the tracked ball and 0.1 ms steps 4 cm, so the step length causes most of the miss in height.

Fitted to 16 frames, 0.1 ms steps call the shot clean, like the tracked ball, and 40 ms steps call it a rim hit.
