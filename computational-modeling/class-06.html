Today we work the problem on the 12 short records in the example pack (test 9793), the same records the example reports use.

Each phrase in the request asks for something we have to work out from the record.

Bob knows the game, so he leaves out what a basketball player takes for granted.

The request saysWhat it takes to read itSo he means
“before it gets there” The short records stop a few frames after the release, long before the hoop. The crossing has to come from the fitted model, since no frame shows it.
“comes down through the height of the rim” An arc passes through every height below its top twice, once rising and once falling. The falling crossing of 3.048 m, the rim height in the header.
“when and where” The record gives the ball’s centre along and across the court, and its height, at each frame. The time of the crossing, and the ball’s centre measured from the hoop’s centre at that time.
“whether it goes in” The header gives the radius of the hoop and the radius of the ball. In, out, or on the rim, decided by how far the ball’s centre is from the hoop’s centre.
“too close to call from the frames you have” A fit to a few frames can move when the frames change. Each call comes with how far its crossing could move.

nextWhen does the fitted ball reach the rim? Write the condition as a root.

Subtracting the rim height turns “when is the ball at the rim” into a root, and the quadratic formula gives it exactly.

Step 4 of 8 · When is the ball at rim height?

record 158, gravity heldz = −2.643 + 11.811 t − 4.905 t² The fit to its 9 flight frames. The top of the arc comes at 1.20 s and 4.47 m.
height above the rim−4.905 t² + 11.811 t − 5.691 = 0 Subtract 3.048 from z. This is a quadratic with a = −4.905, b = 11.811 and c = −5.691, so the formula gives both roots with no starting guess.
$$t = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
what we foundTwo roots, 0.666 s and 1.742 s The later one is 0.74 s after the last frame. Across the 12 records the falling crossing comes 0.74 to 1.05 s after the last frame.

what we learnThe lines run 4.8 m past the data The ball crosses the floor at 6.16 m/s, so a crossing time off by 0.01 s puts it 6 cm long or short. A path turned by 1° misses by 8 cm at the hoop.

A ball coming straight down goes in cleanly when its centre is within 0.109 m of the hoop’s centre, and misses the rim beyond 0.348 m.

Step 6 of 8 · Where does it come down, and does it go in?

in0.2286 − 0.1195 = 0.109 m Hoop radius minus ball radius. Inside this, no part of the ball reaches the rim.
out0.2286 + 0.1195 = 0.348 m Hoop radius plus ball radius. Outside this, no part of the ball reaches the rim.
what we found3 in, 4 on the rim, 5 out The 12 crossings by distance alone. A ball on the rim may go either way.
