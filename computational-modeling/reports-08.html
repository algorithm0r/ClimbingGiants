Example reports · Assignment 7

These are three reports for Assignment 7, Occupant Displacement, written in three different styles. All three model the same example pack (crash test 9793, which no phrase assigns), so nothing here answers anybody's assignment. The three students chose different methods, filters and steps, and each one says why. Their answers agree to within a fraction of a millimeter (110 mm), which is probably the most useful thing the three of them show together. Please see the Example reports page for what every report needs and who it is written for.

Each report is followed by a short note on what it does well. The purpose of the notes is to show that three different routes can meet the same standard (the answer first, evidence that it has settled, assumptions noted). It is a good idea to compare how each one chose its step, since Alice asks for that and the chosen steps range over a factor of 4 (0.05 to 0.2 ms).

A Memo

Example 1 · Memo

To: Alice

Re: How far the occupant moves relative to the car

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

The occupant moves 110 mm forward relative to the car, furthest at 73 ms after impact, and then comes back. That is your model (75 kg, a belt of 296 kN/m and 2.8 kN·s/m of damping) driven by the car's motion in test 9793, a 2017 Hyundai Elantra run into a rigid barrier at 56.15 km/h. The number has settled to much better than a millimeter, and Figure 1 shows the whole movement.

Evidence that it has settled. I stepped the model at the record's own sample interval of 0.05 ms (the logger took 20,000 samples a second), and then at half and a quarter of that. The travel came out at 110.144, 110.081 and 110.050 mm. Each halving moved it half as far as the one before (0.063 mm, then 0.031 mm), which is what forward Euler should do, since its error is proportional to the step. That puts the error at 0.05 ms at about twice the first change, 0.13 mm, and the settled travel at about 110.02 mm. At the 110 mm I am quoting, the step makes no difference.

The step. I used 0.05 ms because at the record's own interval every value of the car's acceleration the method needs is a recorded sample. At the two smaller steps the values in between are read off a straight line joining the neighboring samples.

The method. The occupant is one mass on a spring and a damper (the belt). Its state is two numbers, the travel and the rate of travel, and forward Euler moves both ahead one step at a time from their rates. What drives it is the car's acceleration, filtered at CFC 180. A CFC is the standard crash-data filter from SAE J211, and its class number sets how much fast wiggle it takes out. J211 gives class 180 to structure whose acceleration is going to be integrated, which is what the model does with it. Figure 2 shows the record before and after.

Three things need your attention before this goes to the customer.

  • The car's motion is one sensor. The channel named vehicle_x is an accelerometer on the left rear seat crossmember, according to its header. I have treated it as the motion of the whole car. A car that pitches or crumples unevenly would move the seat differently from that one point.
  • After the peak the model pushes the occupant back past where they started. The travel reaches −42.9 mm at 127.8 ms. A belt can only pull, so that part of the curve is the model's spring pushing, and in the car it would be the seat back doing it. It comes after the 110 mm and does not change it.
  • What the model leaves out. The occupant is one rigid mass, so the head, chest and pelvis all travel the same distance. The belt is a straight-line spring with no slack. There is no airbag or steering wheel, and the occupant moves only forward and back. Each of these would probably change the number, and I have not checked by how much.

Assumptions. The occupant starts at rest relative to the car with the belt just snug, at the start of the record (50 ms before impact). Forward is positive. Your three values are used as given. Between two samples the car's acceleration is a straight line.

Program. a7_belt_travel.py (attached) filters the record, runs the model at the three steps, prints the travel for each and draws both figures.

What to notice

The first sentence gives Alice the travel, when it happens and that it comes back. The settling evidence is three numbers, with the reason they shrink as they do (Euler's change should halve with each halving). The memo says why it picked its step in two sentences. The list keeps what needs action apart from what the model leaves out, and it says which flags do not move the answer.

A Technical Report

Example 2 · Technical report

Data phrase: example pack (test 9793)

Summary

The occupant travels 110.0 mm forward relative to the car, reaching it 73.4 ms after impact, before the belt pulls them back. This is for a 75 kg occupant on a belt of 296 kN/m with 2.8 kN·s/m of damping. The number has settled. At the step used (0.2 ms), two successive halvings each move it by less than 0.001 mm, and the choice of filter on the car's acceleration moves it by less than 0.01 mm. The model is one mass on a linear belt, and what it leaves out is listed under Assumptions and Limits.

Data

Test 9793 is a New Car Assessment test of a 2017 Hyundai Elantra, run head on into a rigid barrier at 56.15 km/h (15.60 m/s). The car's motion comes from channel vehicle_x (code 10SEATLERE00ACXP), which its header describes as the left rear seat crossmember X acceleration. It holds 7001 samples every 0.05 ms (a sample rate of 20 kHz), from 50 ms before impact to 300 ms after. Its largest value is −195.7 g at 52.75 ms (one g is 9.81 m/s²). After filtering at CFC 180 (see Method) the largest value is −51.9 g at 53.35 ms. Figure 1 shows both. The driver's chest X acceleration is used for one check.

Method

The occupant is one mass m held to the car by a spring of stiffness k and a damper c. Writing u for the occupant's travel relative to the car (forward positive), Newton's second law in the car's frame is m u″ + c u′ + k u = −m acar(t). This is stepped as a state of two numbers, the travel u and its rate w, whose rates are w and (−c w − k u)/m − acar. The occupant starts at rest at the start of the record.

The state is advanced with RK4, the fourth-order Runge-Kutta method. It evaluates the rates four times per step, and its error falls by a factor of about 16 each time the step is halved. RK4 makes a strict settling test cheap, which is the reason for using it. The car's acceleration between two samples is a straight line between them. The travel the program reports is the largest value on the step grid, which can only be at or below the true peak, and the step study shows how much that matters.

Filter. The crossmember is filtered with the CFC (channel frequency class) filter of SAE J211-1, run forward and then backward. The course standards page gives vehicle structure CFC 180 when it is to be integrated to velocity or displacement. This model integrates it, so CFC 180 is used. Check 1 shows the choice moves the travel by less than 0.01 mm.

Step. The program runs the model at nine steps, halving from 6.4 ms (128 samples) to 0.025 ms (half a sample), with both the filtered and the raw drive (Table 1 and Figure 2). The step used is the largest one for which the next two halvings each move the travel by less than 0.001 mm. One small change can be luck, which is why two are required. The threshold is a hundredth of the 0.1 mm the travel is quoted to. The step this picks is 0.2 ms (4 samples). Its product with the belt's natural frequency in radians per second is 0.013, far inside the 2.785 at which RK4 becomes unstable (from the stability notes).

The program is a7_occupant_model.py, listed in the appendix and submitted with this report.

With the filtered drive the travel is within 2 mm of its final value even at 6.4 ms, and from 0.2 ms on it changes in the fourth decimal place. The raw drive does not settle until the step is 0.1 ms or smaller. Larger steps skip samples, and the raw record's narrow spikes make the result depend on which samples a run happens to land on (at 1.6 ms it is 19 mm low). The filter therefore matters for the step that can be used, and hardly at all for the answer.

At 0.2 ms the travel is 110.02 mm at 73.4 ms (Figure 3). The occupant then moves back, crosses zero and reaches −42.7 mm at 127.8 ms, and is at 1.3 mm when the record ends at 300 ms. The belt and occupant together have a natural frequency of 10.00 Hz (a period of 100 ms) and a damping ratio of 0.297. The time from the largest forward travel to the largest backward one, 54 ms, is about half that period.

Checks

1. The filter. At the record's own step (0.05 ms) the travel is 110.0185 mm with the CFC 180 drive, 110.0131 mm with CFC 60 and 110.0184 mm with the raw record. The spread is about 0.005 mm. This is probably because the belt responds at 10 Hz, and both filters leave anything that slow alone (a CFC's class number is roughly the frequency below which it passes a signal unchanged).

2. The drive. Integrated over the whole record, the crossmember's acceleration gives a change of velocity of 17.43 m/s against an approach of 15.60 m/s. The difference, 1.83 m/s, is the car rebounding off the barrier. The drive therefore has the right sign and about the right size.

3. The force on the occupant. No channel measures the travel itself, so the model's own acceleration of the occupant (the belt force divided by the mass) is compared with the driver's chest accelerometer, filtered at CFC 180 as J211 gives for the thorax. The model peaks at −48.4 g at 66.4 ms and the chest at −50.4 g at 71.5 ms (Figure 4). The two are close in size and about 5 ms apart. This suggests the model's belt force is of the right size. It does not test the travel directly. After the peak the two part company. The model pushes the occupant backward at up to +20.4 g at 117.6 ms, while the chest never goes above +4.7 g anywhere in the record (at 283.2 ms). The push back is the model's spring, which a belt does not have (see Assumptions and Limits).

Errors and Anomalies

The drive channel's name. The key vehicle_x suggests the whole car, while the header describes one accelerometer on the left rear seat crossmember. This report uses it as the car's motion (see Assumptions) and labels it by its header.

The spike near 53 ms. The raw crossmember reaches −195.7 g at 52.75 ms, nearly four times the filtered peak. It is kept. It barely moves the travel at a fine step (Check 1), and it is the reason the raw drive needs a step of 0.1 ms or smaller.

Chest displacement. The pack's chest displacement channel can look like the answer to this question. It measures how far the chest is compressed, which is a different thing from the occupant's travel, and its unit label is wrong (Assignment 1). It is not used.

Assumptions and Limits

  • The crossmember's motion is the car's motion, and the seat moves with it.
  • The occupant starts at rest relative to the car with the belt just snug, 50 ms before impact.
  • The mass, stiffness and damping are the restraint group's values, used as given.
  • The belt is linear and can push as well as pull. The negative travel after 100 ms is the spring pushing, which a belt cannot do. In the car the seat back would stop the occupant there. This comes after the maximum and does not change it.
  • The occupant is one rigid mass moving only forward and back. The head, chest and pelvis travel different distances in a real occupant.
  • There is no airbag or steering wheel, and no slack in the belt. Each would change the number, by amounts this model cannot estimate.

Appendix. Program

"""Assignment 7: occupant travel relative to the car, with a step study.

Run from the unzipped pack folder. Model: one mass (the occupant) on a spring
and damper (the belt), driven by the car's acceleration,

    m u'' + c u' + k u = -m a_car(t)

with u the travel relative to the car (forward positive). The state (u, w = u')
is stepped with classical RK4. a_car is the crossmember channel filtered at
CFC 180, the class J211 gives a structural acceleration that will be
integrated, and between samples it is a straight line between neighbors.

Prints a table of travel against step for the filtered and the raw drive,
picks the step, then runs three checks. Saves travel.svg, drive.svg,
steps.svg and chest_check.svg.
"""
import json
import os

import numpy as np
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

M = 75.0        # occupant mass, kg
K = 296000.0    # belt stiffness, N/m
C = 2800.0      # belt damping, N*s/m
G = 9.81        # m/s^2 per g
V0 = 15.60      # approach speed, m/s (56.15 km/h from the test record)
SETTLED = 0.001  # mm, a hundredth of the 0.1 mm I quote (RK4 makes this cheap)


def cfc(y, dt, cls):
    """SAE J211-1 Butterworth, forward then backward (assignment 4)."""
    wd = 2 * np.pi * cls * 2.0775
    wa = np.tan(wd * dt / 2)
    d = 1 + np.sqrt(2) * wa + wa**2
    a0 = wa**2 / d
    a1 = 2 * a0
    a2 = a0
    b1 = -2 * (wa**2 - 1) / d
    b2 = (-1 + np.sqrt(2) * wa - wa**2) / d

    def one_pass(x):
        out = np.zeros_like(x)
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            out[i] = a0*x[i] + a1*x[i-1] + a2*x[i-2] + b1*out[i-1] + b2*out[i-2]
        return out

    forward = one_pass(y)
    backward = one_pass(forward[::-1])
    return backward[::-1]


def rk4(t, a_car, h):
    """Step the state (u, w) from rest at t[0] to the end of the record with RK4.
    Returns the step times, u and w."""
    def rate(time, u, w):
        a = np.interp(time, t, a_car)        # straight line between samples
        return w, (-C * w - K * u) / M - a

    n_steps = int(round((t[-1] - t[0]) / h))
    times = t[0] + h * np.arange(n_steps + 1)
    u = np.zeros(n_steps + 1)
    w = np.zeros(n_steps + 1)
    for n in range(n_steps):
        tn = times[n]
        un = u[n]
        wn = w[n]
        k1u, k1w = rate(tn, un, wn)
        k2u, k2w = rate(tn + h/2, un + h/2 * k1u, wn + h/2 * k1w)
        k3u, k3w = rate(tn + h/2, un + h/2 * k2u, wn + h/2 * k2w)
        k4u, k4w = rate(tn + h, un + h * k3u, wn + h * k3w)
        u[n+1] = un + h/6 * (k1u + 2*k2u + 2*k3u + k4u)
        w[n+1] = wn + h/6 * (k1w + 2*k2w + 2*k3w + k4w)
    return times, u, w


def travel(t, a_car, h):
    """Largest forward travel (mm) and when it happens (ms), at step h. This is
    the largest value on the step grid, so it can only be at or below the true
    peak; the step study shows how much that matters."""
    times, u, w = rk4(t, a_car, h)
    i = np.argmax(u)
    return 1000 * u[i], 1000 * times[i]


with open(os.path.join('crash', os.listdir('crash')[0])) as f:
    test = json.load(f)
ch = test['channels']['vehicle_x']
dt = ch['dt']
t = ch['t0'] + dt * np.arange(ch['n'])
raw = np.array(ch['y'])
a180 = cfc(raw, dt, 180)
print(f"drive: {ch['comment']} [{ch['code']}], "
      f"{ch['n']} samples every {1000 * dt} ms")
print(f"peak raw {np.min(raw) / G:.1f} g at {1000 * t[np.argmin(raw)]:.2f} ms, "
      f"CFC 180 {np.min(a180) / G:.1f} g at {1000 * t[np.argmin(a180)]:.2f} ms")
fn = np.sqrt(K / M) / (2 * np.pi)
print(f"belt natural frequency {fn:.2f} Hz (period {1000 / fn:.0f} ms), "
      f"damping ratio {C / (2 * np.sqrt(K * M)):.3f}")

# ---- step study: halve from 6.4 ms (128 samples) down to 0.025 ms (half a sample)
steps = []
for k in range(9):
    steps.append(128 * dt / 2**k)
results = {'CFC 180': [], 'raw': []}
print(f"\n{'step (ms)':>10} {'CFC 180 (mm)':>12} {'change':>9} "
      f"{'raw (mm)':>10} {'change':>9}")
drives = {'CFC 180': a180, 'raw': raw}
for k in range(len(steps)):
    h = steps[k]
    row = f"{1000 * h:10.4f}"
    for name, a in drives.items():
        x, at = travel(t, a, h)
        results[name].append(x)
        if k == 0:
            row = row + f" {x:12.4f} {'':>9}"
        else:
            row = row + f" {x:12.4f} {x - results[name][k-1]:+9.4f}"
    print(row)

# the step: the largest one where the next TWO halvings each move the CFC 180
# travel less than SETTLED (a single small change can be luck)
s180 = results['CFC 180']
for k in range(len(steps) - 2):
    first_change = abs(s180[k+1] - s180[k])
    second_change = abs(s180[k+2] - s180[k+1])
    if first_change < SETTLED and second_change < SETTLED:
        break                      # k is the step chosen
h = steps[k]

      f"{s180[k+1] - s180[k]:+.4f} and {s180[k+2] - s180[k+1]:+.4f} mm")

times, u, w = rk4(t, a180, h)
i = np.argmax(u)
top = u[i]
when = times[i]
j = np.argmin(u)

print(f"most negative travel {1000 * u[j]:.1f} mm at {1000 * times[j]:.1f} ms, "

# ---- checks
print("\nchecks")
print("1. the drive filtered three ways, all at the record's own step:")
checks = {'CFC 180': a180, 'CFC 60': cfc(raw, dt, 60), 'raw': raw}
for name, a in checks.items():
    x, at = travel(t, a, dt)

The occupant on the belt swings at 10 Hz, with a period of 100 ms. A 10 Hz system should not respond much to spikes that last a fraction of a millisecond. For that reason I am using the record as measured, with no filter, and I will check that at the end.

For the stepping I picked Heun's method. It takes an Euler step as a guess, works out the rates at the end of that step, and averages them with the rates at the start. Its error should drop by about four each time the step is halved. The state is the travel u and its rate w, as in the state-as-a-vector notes. For a first try I took a step of 0.4 ms.

def heun(h, a):
    # travel u (m) and its rate w (m/s) relative to the car, from rest at the start
    times = np.arange(t[0], t[-1], h)
    u = np.zeros(len(times))
    w = np.zeros(len(times))
    for n in range(len(times) - 1):
        a1 = np.interp(times[n], t, a)        # car acceleration now
        a2 = np.interp(times[n] + h, t, a)    # and one step later
        du1 = w[n]
        dw1 = (-c * w[n] - k * u[n]) / m - a1
        ug = u[n] + h * du1                   # Euler guess at the end of the step
        wg = w[n] + h * dw1
        du2 = wg
        dw2 = (-c * wg - k * ug) / m - a2
        u[n+1] = u[n] + h / 2 * (du1 + du2)   # average the two rates
        w[n+1] = w[n] + h / 2 * (dw1 + dw2)
    return times, u

Last, the filter check. I copied the CFC filter from assignment 4 and ran the same model with the car filtered at CFC 180, the class J211 gives to structure that is going to be integrated.

def cfc(y, dt, cls):       # from assignment 4
    wd = 2 * np.pi * cls * 2.0775
    wa = np.tan(wd * dt / 2)
    d = 1 + np.sqrt(2) * wa + wa**2
    a0 = wa**2 / d
    b1 = -2 * (wa**2 - 1) / d
    b2 = (-1 + np.sqrt(2) * wa - wa**2) / d

    def one_pass(x):
        out = np.zeros_like(x)
        for i in range(2, len(x)):
            out[i] = a0*x[i] + 2*a0*x[i-1] + a0*x[i-2] + b1*out[i-1] + b2*out[i-2]
        return out

    forward = one_pass(y)
    backward = one_pass(forward[::-1])
    return backward[::-1]

a180 = cfc(a_car, car['dt'], 180)
times, u180 = heun(0.00005, a180)
print('CFC 180 drive:', round(1000 * np.max(u180), 4), 'mm')
print('raw drive:', round(1000 * np.max(u), 4), 'mm')
print('difference', round(1000 * (np.max(u180) - np.max(u)), 4), 'mm')
CFC 180 drive: 110.0186 mm
raw drive: 110.0185 mm
difference 0.0001 mm

Answer. 110 mm, furthest at 73 ms, Heun at a step of 0.05 ms. Halving the step moves it by less than 0.0001 mm. Flags. The car channel is one sensor on the left rear seat crossmember. Its spikes make steps coarser than 0.1 ms unreliable with the raw record. The model pushes the occupant back to −43 mm after the peak, which a belt cannot do. Assumptions. The crossmember moves like the whole car. The occupant starts at rest with the belt snug at −50 ms. Alice's three values are used as given. The car's acceleration between samples is a straight line. Left out. The occupant is one rigid mass moving only forward and back, the belt is a linear spring with no slack, and there is no airbag or steering wheel.

What to notice

The notebook shows a first step that turned out to be too coarse, and it keeps that run in view. The student notices that the change shrank by half where Heun should give a quarter, and offers a reason with a hedge. The choice to leave the record unfiltered is made early, defended, and then tested in the last cell. The answer, flags and assumptions are gathered at the end so a reader does not have to find them in the middle.
