Computational Physical Modeling

Truncation error, from the formula

From lecture 9, the central difference is the derivative plus a truncation error.

$$\frac{f(x+h) - f(x-h)}{2h} \;=\; \underbrace{f'(x)}_{\text{kept}} \;+\; \underbrace{\textcolor{#8A6508}{\tfrac{h^2}{6}f'''(x) + \dots}}_{\text{truncated}}$$

The first dropped term predicts the measured error.

Dots: measured error. Dashed: $\tfrac{h^2}{6}\cos 1$, the first dropped term for $\sin x$.

Rounding error, from the machine

The closer the two weighings, the less of the answer survives.

A bathroom scale that reads to 0.1 kg.

At $h = 10^{-12}$ the derivative keeps only 3 correct digits.

A double (the standard number in Python and MATLAB) keeps about 16 significant digits.

$$\begin{aligned} \sin(1+h) &= 0.\textcolor{#b9b0a0}{84147098480}843685\\ \sin(1-h) &= 0.\textcolor{#b9b0a0}{84147098480}735627\\[4pt] \text{difference} &= 0.000000000001080580\\[4pt] \text{difference} \div 2h &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{0.54029}00\ldots \qquad \cos 1 = 0.5403023\ldots \end{aligned}$$

A fixed storage error divided by $2h$ grows as $h$ shrinks.

Each stored sine is off by about $10^{-16}$, whatever the step.

Computational Physical Modeling

The step size that makes the total error smallest

The total error is truncation plus rounding, so it has a minimum.

Drawn for $\sin x$ at $x = 1$. Other functions shift the two lines.

$$E(h) \;\approx\; \underbrace{C h^2}_{\text{truncation}} \;+\; \underbrace{\frac{\varepsilon |f|}{h}}_{\text{rounding}}$$

At the minimum, the slope of $E$ is zero.

Differentiate each term with the power rule.

$$\begin{aligned} E(h) &= C h^2 \;+\; \varepsilon|f|\,h^{-1}\\[6pt] \frac{dE}{dh} &= 2Ch \;-\; \varepsilon|f|\,h^{-2} \;=\; \textcolor{#8A6508}{2Ch - \frac{\varepsilon|f|}{h^2}} \end{aligned}$$

Setting the slope to zero and solving gives the best step.

$$\begin{aligned} 2Ch &= \frac{\varepsilon|f|}{h^2} &&\text{the two terms balance}\\[6pt] 2C\,h^3 &= \varepsilon|f| &&\text{multiply both sides by } h^2\\[6pt] h_{\text{best}} &= \textcolor{#8A6508}{\left(\frac{\varepsilon|f|}{2C}\right)^{1/3}} &&\text{divide by } 2C\text{, take the cube root} \end{aligned}$$

For $\sin x$ at $x = 1$, the best step size is about $10^{-5}$.

$$\begin{aligned} C &= \tfrac{1}{6}\cos 1 = 0.0901 & |f| &= \sin 1 = 0.841\\[6pt] \varepsilon|f| &= (2.2\times10^{-16})(0.841) = 1.87\times10^{-16} & 2C &= 0.180\\[6pt] \frac{\varepsilon|f|}{2C} &= 1.04\times10^{-15} & h_{\text{best}} &= (1.04\times10^{-15})^{1/3} = \textcolor{#8A6508}{1.0\times10^{-5}} \end{aligned}$$
