Integrating sampled data

The definite integral is the signed area under a curve.

$$\int_a^b f(x)\,\mathrm{d}x$$

We only have samples of the curve.

So fill each gap with a shape whose area you know.

The gap between two samples is a panel, of width $h$.

  • n + 1 samples so n gaps between them
  • h the width of one gap, which is the sample spacing
  • the sum the areas of n shapes, one per gap

Computational Physical Modeling

The rectangle rule

A rectangle, flat at the left-hand sample.

$$A = h\,f(x_i)$$

Add the areas.

$$\int_a^b f \,\mathrm{d}x \;\approx\; h\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} f(x_i)$$

Flat tops sit under a rising curve, over a falling one.

The trapezoid rule

Use both ends, and join them with a straight line.

$$A = \frac{h}{2}\bigl(f(x_i) + f(x_{i+1})\bigr)$$

A trapezoid is a rectangle plus a triangle.

$$\begin{aligned} A &= h\,f(x_i) + \tfrac12\,h\bigl(f(x_{i+1}) - f(x_i)\bigr)\\[0.4em] &= h\,f(x_i) + \tfrac12 h\,f(x_{i+1}) - \tfrac12 h\,f(x_i)\\[0.4em] &= \frac{h}{2}\bigl(f(x_i) + f(x_{i+1})\bigr) \end{aligned}$$

Two panels share a sample, and its halves make one.

$$\begin{aligned} A_i &= \tfrac{h}{2}\bigl(f(x_i) + \textcolor{#8A6508}{f(x_{i+1})}\bigr)\\[0.3em] A_{i+1} &= \tfrac{h}{2}\bigl(\textcolor{#8A6508}{f(x_{i+1})} + f(x_{i+2})\bigr)\\[0.7em] A_i + A_{i+1} &= h\Bigl[\tfrac12 f(x_i) + \textcolor{#8A6508}{f(x_{i+1})} + \tfrac12 f(x_{i+2})\Bigr] \end{aligned}$$

Interior samples count twice, and the two ends count once.

$$h\!\left[\tfrac12 f(x_0) + f(x_1) + \cdots + f(x_{n-1}) + \tfrac12 f(x_n)\right]$$

The whole difference between the rules is one term.

$$\begin{aligned} R &= h\bigl[\,\textcolor{#8A6508}{\phantom{\tfrac12}f(x_0)} + f(x_1) + \cdots + f(x_{n-1})\,\bigr]\\[0.5em] T &= h\bigl[\,\textcolor{#8A6508}{\tfrac12 f(x_0)} + f(x_1) + \cdots + f(x_{n-1}) + \textcolor{#8A6508}{\tfrac12 f(x_n)}\,\bigr]\\[1.1em] T - R &= \frac{h}{2}\bigl(\textcolor{#8A6508}{f(x_n) - f(x_0)}\bigr) \end{aligned}$$

$x^3 - 3x + 3$ splits into four sections of slope and curvature.

Choose a rule and a number of panels.

The slope sets the sign of the rectangle error.

  • rising the flat top lies below the curve, so the answer comes out low
  • falling the flat top lies above it, so the answer comes out high

The curvature sets the sign of the trapezoid error.

  • concave the chord lies below the curve, so the answer comes out low
  • convex the chord lies above it, so the answer comes out high

Accuracy on a known integral

$\sin x$ from 0 to $\pi/2$ has an area of exactly 1.

$$\int_0^{\pi/2}\!\sin x\,\mathrm{d}x = \Big[-\cos x\Big]_0^{\pi/2} = \textcolor{#8A6508}{1}$$

At 16 panels, rectangle error is 5.0% and trapezoid error 0.08%.

So rectangle error goes as $h$, and trapezoid error as $h^2$.

$$\text{rectangle} \sim h \qquad\qquad \text{trapezoid} \sim \textcolor{#8A6508}{h^2}$$

On log axes, the slope is the exponent.
