Solution 1 — Logic and Sets

Problems

You, Alice, and Bob have been hired by TooMuchMoney Corp to select 8 scholarship recipients from 32 students enrolled in the School of Engineering and Technology at the University of Washington Tacoma. The company wants "the best students" but also "a diverse group." It is typical for database users to need to translate natural language requests like this into logical and set theoretic statements.

The database contains several fields that could be relevant for determining "best" and "diverse" students. For academic merit and diversity considerations, you have access to gpa, credits, year, honors, research, major, state, first_gen, international, transfer, double_major, internship and club_leadership. For anonymizing purposes you have access to no other information.

Let's start by considering how you will select the "best" students. You will need to select some criteria that you think is fair to select which students deserve the scholarships the most based on merit.

  1. Write an explanation in English of the criteria you will apply to determine which students are the "best". For example, you might write "students with GPA of 4.0" as part of your criteria. Try to be clear and logically precise with your language. Justify why you have selected these criteria.

    Solution: First, I will take GPA as the primary indicator of academic talent, and will try to select students with a GPA above 3.5 to capture this. However, GPA is not the only criteria that makes a good student. I also want to emphasize participation in extracurricular activities like research, honors, internship, a double major, or club leadership. I will want selected students to have at least one of these traits.

  2. Now use set builder notation to describe the set of students that would be selected by your criteria. Try to use mathematical notation whenever possible to replace your natural language.

    Solution: I have defined two sets of students. Let $S$ be the set of all potential scholarship recipients.

    $$GPA = \{s \in S \mid s\text{ has a GPA }\geq 3.5\} $$

    and

    $$\begin{aligned} EC = \{s \in S \mid &\text{research(s) = TRUE} \\ &\text{ or honors(s) = TRUE} \\ &\text{ or internship(s) = TRUE} \\ &\text{ or double\_major(s) = TRUE} \\ &\text{ or club\_leadership(s) = TRUE} \\ \} \end{aligned}$$

    I want to select students from the intersection of these sets.

    $$SEL = GPA \cap EC $$

Now consider the criteria you will use for determining if your selected students represent a "diverse" group. Often this means defining mutually exclusive sets that you want to find representatives from each.

  1. State in English the sets you want to consider when evaluating the selected students for diversity. For instance, you might want to ensure at least one student from each year gets a scholarship. Try to be clear and logically precise with your language. Justify why you have selected these criteria.

    Solution: To select a diverse group of students I want to represent different demographics. For instance, I want to give scholarships (if possible) to a student of every year, every major, both in-state and out-of-state students, both international and domestic students, both transfer and non-transfer students, and both first-gen and non-first-gen students.

  2. Now use set builder notation to describe the different sets of students that you will use to evaluate your scholarship recipients for "diversity". Try to use mathematical notation whenever possible to replace your natural language.

    Solution: Selecting students by year creates four disjoint sets of students:

    $$\begin{aligned} &Y1 = \{s \in S \mid \text{year(s) = 1}\} \\ &Y2 = \{s \in S \mid \text{year(s) = 2}\} \\ &Y3 = \{s \in S \mid \text{year(s) = 3}\} \\ &Y4 = \{s \in S \mid \text{year(s) = 4}\} \end{aligned}$$

    and selecting by major creates five disjoint sets:

    $$\begin{aligned} &CS = \{s \in S \mid \text{major(s) = CS}\} \\ &CE = \{s \in S \mid \text{major(s) = CE}\} \\ &EE = \{s \in S \mid \text{major(s) = EE}\} \\ &ME = \{s \in S \mid \text{major(s) = ME}\} \\ &IT = \{s \in S \mid \text{major(s) = IT}\} \end{aligned}$$

    Each of the other traits splits the students into two disjoint sets:

    $$\begin{aligned} &STATE = \{s \in S \mid \text{state(s) = WA}\} \\ &\overline{STATE} = \{s \in S \mid \text{state(s) != WA}\} \\ &INT = \{s \in S \mid \text{international(s) = TRUE}\} \\ &\overline{INT} = \{s \in S \mid \text{international(s) = FALSE}\} \\ &TRANSFER = \{s \in S \mid \text{transfer(s) = TRUE}\} \\ &\overline{TRANSFER} = \{s \in S \mid \text{transfer(s) = FALSE}\} \\ &FIRST\text{-}GEN = \{s \in S \mid \text{first\_gen(s) = TRUE}\} \\ &\overline{FIRST\text{-}GEN} = \{s \in S \mid \text{first\_gen(s) = FALSE}\} \end{aligned}$$

Now consider the students listed in the table below. Use the criteria you determined to select 8 students for the scholarship.

  1. Which 8 students would you select for the scholarships?

    Solution: I will award scholarships to students:

    • 003 (GPA 3.22, 3rd Year IT, Out-of-State, 1st-Gen, Transfer)
    • 031 (GPA 3.38, 2nd Year CS, Out-of-State, 1st-Gen, Transfer)
    • 025 (GPA 3.88, 4th Year ME, Transfer)
    • 004 (GPA 3.85, 3rd Year CS, Transfer)
    • 001 (GPA 3.98, 4th Year CS, International, Transfer)
    • 030 (GPA 3.92, 1st Year CS, International)
    • 017 (GPA 3.93, 4th Year CE, Transfer)
    • 007 (GPA 3.91, 4th Year EE, International)

  2. Explain the reasoning process you used to select these 8 students and try to make reference to the criteria you developed above.

    Solution: The set

    $$\begin{aligned} GPA = \{&001, 002, 004, 006, 007, 009, 010, 012, 014, 017, \\ &018, 020, 021, 023, 025, 027, 028, 030, 032\} \end{aligned}$$

    has 19 members and the set

    $$\begin{aligned} EC = \{&001, 002, 003, 004, 006, 007, 009, 010, 011, 012, 014, 015, 017, \\ &018, 019, 020, 021, 024, 025, 027, 028, 030, 031, 032\} \end{aligned}$$

    has 24 members. Intersecting them we get

    $$\begin{aligned} GPA \cap EC = \{&001, 002, 004, 006, 007, 009, 010, 012, 014, 017, \\ &018, 020, 021, 025, 027, 028, 030, 032\} \end{aligned}$$

    with 18 members. I'll start with this set and attempt to select my diverse group of students by iterating in order of GPA from highest to lowest. Putting the students in order of GPA results in the following table.

    My initial goal is to select the top 8 students, but this presents problems for my diversity goals. For instance, the top 8 students are all from Washington, and thus are in-state students. They also don't represent all majors or years. In fact, looking at our list, we have no students from year 2 nor from IT. This poses a dilemma: proceed with this set of students or return to the original set to select additional students. Because I value the diversity goals above the quality criteria I choose to return to the set to pull more students out.

    The IT student with highest GPA is 003 with GPA 3.22 and they meet the extracurricular requirement. The 2nd year student with the highest GPA is 031 with GPA 3.38 and they also meet the extracurricular requirement. Adding these two to our list of recipients ensures diversity in these categories.

    If we take these two with the students with top six GPA we still miss out on ME and 3rd year students. So I will select student 025 (ME), and student 004 (3rd year).

    Finally, in order to also include EE students and 1st year students it's important to skip student 002 when selecting by GPA. This results in a set of 8 students that cover our diversity categories while also maximizing overall GPA.

Extra Credit

After the success of your selection criteria the TooMuchMoney Corp has decided to award another four high achievers. They want you to select four students that:

  • either represent the four years (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th) or represent four of the five majors (CS, IT, CE, EE, ME)
  • and all have a GPA at least 3.5.

For each of the options (divided by year or major) either show that the combination is impossible, or show a satisfying selection of students. If you are arguing that a combination is impossible, you must provide an argument that will convince the stubborn executive that is used to getting what they ask for. Otherwise, a sentence or two showing why your students meet the criteria is all you need if a selection satisfying the criteria is possible.
